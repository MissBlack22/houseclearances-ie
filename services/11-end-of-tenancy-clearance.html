End of Tenancy Clearance

Every day a rental property sits uncleared between tenants is a day it's not earning rent. HouseClearances.ie provides end of tenancy clearance, rental property clearance and landlord clearout services across Dublin and Leinster, working to landlords' and letting agents' deadlines so the property is back on the market as fast as possible.

Who This Is For

  • Landlords turning a property around between tenants.
  • Letting agents managing clearances across a portfolio of units.
  • Tenants themselves, arranging a clearance as part of moving out and handing back the keys.

A single-property landlord and a letting agent running a portfolio of units need the same outcome — a clean, lettable handover — but usually arrive at it differently. A landlord letting a property directly is often reacting to one specific tenancy ending and trying to close the gap before the next tenant's rent starts. A letting agent is more often working from a portfolio calendar, with several properties due to turn over in the same month and an expectation that a contractor can simply fit into their process without hand-holding. Tenants who contact us directly are usually doing so to avoid a dispute over the condition a property is left in — clearing what they can't take with them before handover, rather than leaving it for the landlord to sort out afterwards.

What We Clear

Abandoned furniture, rubbish, and general belongings left behind by a previous tenant — full properties or single rooms. We work to get the property to a genuinely lettable standard: cleared, swept, and ready for the next tenant's viewing.

Speed Matters Here

Unlike a standard house clearance, the priority in a tenancy turnaround is almost always the date — a new tenant moving in, a viewing already booked, or an agent needing the keys back. Tell us your deadline when you enquire and we'll confirm what's realistic, including same-week turnarounds where possible.

A property that sits uncleared between tenancies isn't just lost rent for the days it takes to sort — it usually pushes back everything that has to happen after the clearance too: a cleaning crew, a painter touching up marks on the walls, a photographer booked for the new listing, or a compliance check like a BER assessment. If the clearance is the first domino, a delay there delays everyone booked in after it. That's why we treat the deadline as the actual brief rather than a nice-to-have — when you tell us a new tenant moves in on a specific date, or an agent has photos booked for the following week, that's what the job is planned around.

For the most time-pressured turnarounds, we can sometimes manage a same-day end of tenancy clearance in Dublin, depending on what else is already booked in. If your date is genuinely tight, get in touch as early as possible — same-day and next-day availability is limited and goes on a first-come basis.

This is the part of our work that functions as a letting agent clearance service rather than a one-off callout: repeat business built around how your agency actually operates, not a fresh negotiation for every unit.

The admin burden of managing clearances across a portfolio is usually the bigger frustration than any single job — a different quote process each time, a different point of contact, and re-explaining what "ready to re-list" means for your properties on every instruction. A standing arrangement is built to remove that. Consistent pricing matters here too. And because we already know your properties and your priorities, agencies with a standing arrangement get priority scheduling when a turnaround date is tight, rather than joining a general queue behind first-time enquiries.

If you manage properties across Dublin city and county, or further out into Kildare, Wicklow or Carlow, a single point of contact who already knows your process is worth more than it sounds — one phone call or email covers a clearance regardless of which of your properties it's for, rather than starting from scratch with a contractor every time a unit changes hands. House Shares and HMOs

Shared houses and HMOs (houses in multiple occupation) bring their own complication — several tenants' belongings mixed together, sometimes with different move-out dates. Tell us the situation when you enquire and we'll work out a sensible approach, whether that's one clearance once everyone's out or clearing individual rooms as they empty.

In practice, HMO clearance in Dublin tends to fall into a few recurring patterns. Sometimes every tenant leaves on the same date and the whole property needs clearing in one visit, room by room, before new tenants move in together. More often it's staggered — one room empties while two or three others are still occupied, and whatever's left behind in the vacated room needs clearing without disturbing the tenants still living there. We can work a single room at a time in these cases, coordinating the visit so it doesn't intrude on people still resident in the house.

Shared houses also tend to accumulate more communal items than a single-let property does — a kitchen table nobody in particular owns, furniture bought jointly by a group of tenants and left behind when the house share broke up, or belongings from someone who moved out months earlier and never came back for them. Where it isn't clear whose something is, tell us what you know and we'll clear it as part of the job; we're not in a position to establish ownership between former tenants, only to remove what a landlord or agent instructs us to.

Deposit and Handover Deadlines

We understand that a tenancy clearance is often tied to a hard deadline — a deposit return, a handover inspection, or a new tenant's move-in date already booked. Tell us the date that matters and we'll build the schedule around it rather than fitting you in whenever suits us.

Belongings left behind by a departed tenant are one of the most common reasons a clearance gets booked in the first place — furniture nobody wanted, boxes that didn't make the move, or items that were simply forgotten. We clear these as part of the job, sorted for donation, recycling or disposal in the usual way. What we don't do is advise on the legal position around abandoned tenant property — how long it needs to be held, what notice, if any, is required, or what a landlord is entitled to do with it varies by situation, and it's worth confirming your own position separately before anything is disposed of, particularly if there's any chance the previous tenant intends to come back for it. Once that's settled on your end, we handle the physical clearance itself.

What Affects the Cost

We don't work off a flat rate for end of tenancy clearances, because two properties of a similar size can take very different amounts of time depending on what's actually involved. A free end of tenancy clearance quote takes the following into account:

  • Property size. A one-bed flat and a five-bed house clear at very different speeds, and size is usually the starting point for any quote.
  • Volume of items left behind. A tenant who left a few bin bags is a different job to one who left a house full of furniture and belongings — tell us roughly what's involved and we'll scope it accordingly.
  • Access. Ground-floor houses with off-street parking generally clear faster than upper-floor flats or properties with restricted parking; where lifts, stairwells or building rules are the main complication rather than the tenancy deadline itself, see our Apartment Clearance page.
  • Urgency of the deadline. A same-week or same-day turnaround has to be slotted into the schedule differently than a clearance booked weeks in advance, which can affect both availability and price.

We don't publish fixed prices because these factors genuinely change the job — every landlord clearance quote is free, no-obligation, and built around what you actually tell us about the property rather than a generic per-room rate.
