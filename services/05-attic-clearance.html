Attic Clearance

Attics collect whatever a household doesn't want to look at but isn't ready to throw out — boxes from a move that never got unpacked, decorations, old documents, furniture nobody has room for downstairs, and things that have simply been up there so long nobody remembers what's in the box. HouseClearances.ie clears attics across Dublin, including the awkward physical side of the job that a lot of people underestimate until they're halfway up a ladder.

Why Attics Are Their Own Job

Unlike most other rooms, an attic clearance usually means working through a hatch, on a ladder, with low headroom, exposed joists, and no proper flooring in parts of the space. It's genuinely awkward and, done carelessly, genuinely unsafe — stepping through a ceiling is a real risk if you're not used to working in that environment. Our crews handle attic access properly, including moving items safely down through the hatch without damaging ceilings, walls, or stairwells on the way.

Not All Attic Access Is the Same

The type of access shapes how the job runs, so it's worth knowing which one you've got before you call:

  • Fixed staircase with a boarded floor — the easiest case, closer to clearing an ordinary room, though converted attic rooms can still have restricted headroom under the eaves.
  • Pull-down or fold-away ladder — everything has to go up and down a narrow, often unstable ladder one item at a time, which slows the job and rules out awkward or heavy pieces going down in one go.
  • Simple hatch, no fixed ladder at all — the most restrictive setup, usually meaning a step ladder from the landing below and everything passed hand to hand.

Where there's no proper flooring, our crews work off the joists rather than the plasterboard between them, keeping weight distributed to avoid damaging the ceiling of the room below. Boxes and smaller items come down through the hatch by hand; bulkier pieces — an old wardrobe, a cot, stored furniture — sometimes need to be broken down first if they won't clear the opening, which we'll flag as part of the quote rather than as a surprise on the day. Low headroom near the eaves is common in older Dublin roof spaces in particular, and it's simply a case of working carefully and taking the time the space demands rather than rushing it.

What Typically Comes Out

No two attics are the same, but most fall into a few recognisable categories:

  • Boxed belongings — often unopened since a house move, sometimes years or even decades old, and frequently the largest single category by volume
  • Furniture stored out of the way — spare beds, cots, wardrobes, dining chairs, anything downsized rooms no longer have space for
  • Seasonal items — Christmas decorations, camping gear, suitcases only used once or twice a year
  • Paper and documents — old bills, books, school and college files, photo albums
  • Household overflow — sports equipment, toys long outgrown, off-season clothing, spare bedding
  • Odds and ends from past projects — leftover tiles or flooring offcuts, tins of paint, fittings kept "just in case" after a renovation
  • General debris around the storage itself where it's part of the wider clearance — this is not an insulation removal or roof repair service, but loose material disturbed while clearing stored items is cleaned up as we go

Different Attics, Different Jobs

"Attic clearance" covers a wider range of jobs than the name suggests:

  • Converted attic rooms — a proper floor, stairs, sometimes plasterboard walls and a window — usually storing furniture and boxes rather than the general overflow found in an unconverted space, and cleared much like any other room.
  • Unconverted storage attics — bare joists, insulation between them, hatch or ladder access — the more physically demanding version of the job, and the one this page is mostly written around.
  • House-move clearances — clearing an attic before a sale or handover, often against a completion date, where everything needs to be out rather than partially sorted.
  • Downsizing — moving to a smaller home or an apartment with no attic at all, where the question isn't just what to keep but what will actually fit in the new place.
  • Inherited or estate attics — clearing a family home after a bereavement, where an attic can hold decades of a relative's belongings. This is sensitive work by nature; see our dedicated bereavement clearance service for how we handle it.
  • Long-neglected attics — spaces that haven't been opened or sorted through in years, sometimes since a previous owner. These take longer to assess but are handled the same way as any other attic once we know what's up there.

What Affects the Cost

Every attic clearance is quoted individually, but the same handful of factors tend to move the price:

  • Access type — a boarded floor and fixed stairs is quicker to clear than a pull-down ladder or bare hatch, where everything has to be moved by hand in smaller loads.
  • Volume — a handful of boxes is a very different job from an attic packed floor to ridge over twenty years.
  • Whether you want items reviewed first — bringing boxes down for you to check before disposal takes longer than clearing straight to the van, so it's worth telling us in advance if that's what you want.
  • Time on site — awkward access, low headroom, and breaking down oversized furniture to get it through the hatch all add time, which is factored into the quote once we know what we're dealing with.

There's no fixed price list for attic work because access varies so much house to house — the fairest way is to tell us roughly what's up there and how it's reached, and we'll quote from that.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is attic access included, or does it cost extra?

Access is factored into the quote once we know how the attic is reached — a proper staircase and boarded floor is a different job from a pull-down ladder and no flooring. Tell us what you've got and we'll quote accordingly.

Can I look through boxes before you dispose of anything?

Yes — just let us know in advance and we'll bring items down for you to check rather than clearing straight to the van.

Is it safe to clear an attic with no proper flooring?

Our crews are used to working in exactly that kind of space and take the appropriate care — that's part of why this is a dedicated service rather than something we treat as routine as clearing a bedroom.

How do you get items down safely without damaging the house?

Smaller items come down through the hatch by hand. Anything bulkier is walked down carefully to avoid catching walls, door frames, or the stairwell on the way, and broken down first if it won't clear the hatch opening. We work off the joists where there's no proper flooring so weight doesn't rest on the ceiling below.

Do you clear attics with only a pull-down ladder, no proper stairs?

Yes — that's one of the most common setups we deal with. It slows the job down because everything has to go up and down a narrow ladder rather than being carried out in bulk, and we account for that when quoting.

What if the attic hasn't been sorted through in years?

That's a normal starting point for us, not an unusual one. We'll clear it the same way as any other attic — sorted for reuse, recycling, or disposal as agreed — it may just take a bit longer to work through than an attic that's been kept tidy.

Can you clear an attic as part of a house sale or move?

Yes, and we're used to working to a completion date or handover deadline — let us know the timeline when you enquire so we can fit the job around it.
