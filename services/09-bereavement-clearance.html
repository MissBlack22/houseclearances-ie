Bereavement Clearance

Clearing a loved one's home is rarely just a practical task. HouseClearances.ie provides a bereavement clearance service across Dublin built around that reality — unhurried, discreet, and led by what the family needs, not a fixed process we push everyone through.

How We Approach This

There's no single right way to clear a family home after a loss, and we don't assume there is. Some families want everything handled quickly and from a distance; others want to be present, go through belongings themselves first, or set aside specific items before anything else is touched. We take our lead from you.

  • Flexible timing. We work to your timeline, not the other way round — whether that's a quick turnaround for a sale or probate deadline, or no rush at all.
  • Discreet. Our crews carry out the work quietly and respectfully, without treating it as routine.
  • Family-led decisions. Items of sentimental or financial value can be set aside for the family to review before anything is cleared, if that's what you'd prefer.
  • Donation-forward. Where a family would like belongings donated rather than disposed of, we prioritise that — many families find it genuinely helpful that things go to someone who needs them.

Every family approaches this differently, and both ends of that spectrum are equally normal. Some prefer to step back entirely and let us handle everything from start to finish, without having to walk through the rooms themselves. Others want to be there for some or all of it — present while we work, checking in as we go, or asking us to hold off on a particular room until they've had a chance to look through it. Timing varies just as much: a property being sold or handed back to a landlord often comes with a real date to work towards, while other clearances have no deadline attached at all and simply happen whenever the family feels ready. We shape the approach around whichever of these fits your situation, rather than asking you to fit around ours.

Estate and Probate Situations

Bereavement clearances often overlap with probate or estate administration, sometimes coordinated with executors or a solicitor as part of settling the estate. We're happy to liaise directly with whoever is managing the process, and to work to any deadlines tied to a sale or handover of the property. If you've just been named executor, our executor's checklist for Ireland covers the wider admin — probate, Revenue, Capital Acquisitions Tax — with a free downloadable checklist.

In practice, this can mean anything from a single phone call to arrange access and keys, to working alongside a solicitor's office on timing over several weeks. Some executors prefer to leave the property untouched until certain administrative steps are complete; others want the clearance done early simply to reduce the number of things on their list. Neither approach is wrong, and it isn't something we'd advise on either way — that's a conversation for you and your solicitor. Whatever's been decided, we're happy to fit around it, including liaising directly with a solicitor's office if that's easier for the family.

What We Clear

The full contents of a home — furniture, personal effects, clothing, household items, and anything in the garage, shed or attic. See our main House Clearance page for the broader detail on how a full clearance works; this page covers specifically how we approach it in a bereavement context.

Sorting Through Belongings

Before anything is cleared, we set aside anything that looks like it might matter — documents, photographs, jewellery, or small items that are easy to overlook in a full house — so the family has the chance to look through them rather than risk losing something important. If there are particular items you already know you want kept aside, just let us know beforehand and we'll treat them accordingly from the start, no need to search for them once the clearance is underway.

For everything else, our approach is donation-forward wherever a family is comfortable with that. Furniture, clothing, and household items in good condition are passed on to local charities and reuse organisations rather than sent for disposal, so they go to someone who can use them. It's a small thing in the middle of a difficult process, but many families tell us it makes clearing a home feel a little less final.

Responsible Disposal

We're a fully insured waste carrier. Items in good condition are set aside for donation or resale wherever the family is comfortable with that, and we aim for an average recycling rate of up to 95% across everything we clear. What's left after reuse and recycling is disposed of through a licensed waste transfer station.

What Affects the Cost

Every bereavement clearance is priced individually, based on a conversation about your specific situation rather than a fixed rate card, but a few things generally shape the quote: the volume of belongings and the size of the property, how easy the property is to access, and how much flexibility there is around timing. A clearance with a fixed deadline tied to a sale isn't necessarily more expensive, but knowing the timeline upfront helps us plan the right crew and vehicle for the job. We'll always talk through the specifics with you before giving a free, no-obligation quote, so there's nothing unexpected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can we go through the house ourselves before you clear it?

Yes — take whatever time you need. We're happy to arrange the clearance for whenever you're ready, and to set aside anything you'd like to look through first.

Can you work with a solicitor or executor managing the estate?

Yes, we regularly coordinate directly with whoever is handling the process, on timing, access, and any specific instructions for the property.

Can items be donated rather than disposed of?

Yes — if that matters to your family, let us know and we'll prioritise donation for anything in suitable condition.

Is there a rush to book this quickly?

No. Get in touch whenever you're ready — we'll work to your timeline, not the other way round.

Can you clear a property while probate is still ongoing?

Yes, this is common. Many families and executors choose to have a property cleared before probate is fully finalised, particularly when a sale or handover is being planned around it. We're not in a position to advise on whether that's the right timing for your situation — that's a question for your solicitor — but once you and your solicitor have agreed a plan, we're happy to work to it.

What happens to items that are donated?

Anything in good condition that a family is happy to part with is passed on to local charities and reuse organisations rather than sent for disposal. It's part of the up to 95% average recycling rate we aim for across everything we clear.

Do we need to be there while you clear the house?

Not unless you'd like to be. Some families prefer to be present throughout, others give us access and a list of instructions and let us get on with it. Either works, and you're welcome to change your mind partway through if it doesn't feel right on the day.

How much does a bereavement clearance cost?

It depends on the volume of belongings, the size of the property, and how easy it is to access, so we don't quote a flat rate. Get in touch for a free, no-obligation quote and we'll talk through the details with you first.
