Apartment Clearance

Apartments and flats come with their own set of practical problems that a house clearance doesn't: a lift that needs booking in advance, a narrow stairwell, a management company's loading-bay rules, or a resident's parking permit that only covers one bay. HouseClearances.ie clears apartments across Dublin with those constraints built into the plan from the start, not worked around on the day. Whether it's a single flat clearance for a tenant moving out or a landlord managing several units at once, the same rule applies: find out how the building actually works before the van turns up, not after.

Who This Is For

  • Tenants moving out who need a flat emptied and left in a lettable condition.
  • Landlords and letting agents turning a unit around between tenancies, sometimes on a tight deadline.
  • Families clearing an apartment as part of a bereavement or probate process — see our Bereavement Clearance page for how we handle that side of it.
  • Management companies dealing with an abandoned unit or a resident who has passed away with no immediate family locally.

Access, Sorted Properly

Before the day, we ask about the practical details that actually determine how a clearance runs: which floor, whether there's a lift and its size, whether the building requires a loading-bay booking or a specific access window, and where we can legally park to load. Getting this right in advance means the crew isn't standing in a hallway working it out on the day — which matters both for speed and for not annoying the neighbours.

No two apartment buildings work the same way, so we don't plan them the same way either. A period conversion — a Georgian or Victorian house on a Dublin 4 or Dublin 8 terrace split into three or four flats — usually means a narrow, turning staircase, no lift at all, and doorways that weren't built with furniture in mind. That kind of job is planned around stair-carrying: fewer large single items, more trips, and a crew who know how to get a wardrobe or sofa down a half-landing without marking the walls. A modern development is a different problem entirely. There's often a passenger lift for residents and a separate goods or service lift for anything bulky — and buildings that make that distinction usually insist you use the right one, at the right time. Some blocks run a formal loading-bay booking system through the management company or a concierge, with a defined window for deliveries and removals; turn up outside it and you can be turned away. We ask about all of this upfront rather than finding out from a porter on the day.

Parking is its own small logistics problem in most of the areas we work. Resident parking permit schemes, single-bay allocations, and pay-and-display zones around apartment blocks in places like Rathmines, Portobello and Smithfield all affect how close a van can actually get to the door — and that, in turn, affects how long a clearance takes. Where a building has a set-down area or a bay that can be reserved in advance, we'll ask you or the management agent to sort that booking, or coordinate it directly if you'd rather not deal with it. Where management company sign-off is needed before any contractor can work in common areas — some managing agents require this in writing before a lift can even be used for a clearance — we're used to providing whatever insurance or permit details they ask for.

What We Clear

Furniture, appliances (including built-in or freestanding white goods), household contents, and any general accumulation of belongings. In rental turnarounds this often includes items a previous tenant has simply left behind — we clear these the same way as anything else, sorted for donation, recycling, or responsible disposal.

Types of Apartment Clearance

Not every apartment job is the same shape, and we quote them differently because of it.

  • Full flat clearance. Every room, every item — the whole unit emptied down to bare walls and floors. The most common request from landlords, letting agents, and families dealing with a property after a death.
  • Partial or single-room clearance. Sometimes it's one bedroom, a home office, or just the contents of a storage cupboard that needs clearing while the rest of the flat stays as it is. We price and schedule these the same way, just on a smaller scale.
  • End-of-tenancy turnaround. A flat that needs to go from "tenant just left" to "ready to re-list" as fast as possible, often including a clear-out of items left behind. Our End of Tenancy Clearance page covers the turnaround-specific detail — timelines, what typically gets left, and how we work around a tight re-let date.
  • Bereavement and probate apartment clearance. Clearing a flat after a death, often on behalf of an executor or next of kin who may not be local and may be doing this for the first time. This needs a different pace and a different conversation to a rental turnaround — see Bereavement Clearance for how we handle it.
  • Hoarder situations in apartments. A build-up of belongings in a flat brings its own access problems on top of the building's own — blocked hallways, restricted movement inside the unit, and sometimes a need to work floor by floor. Our Hoarder Clearance page goes into how we approach that sensitively and safely.
  • Storage-adjacent clearance. Many Dublin apartment blocks include a private storage locker or basement store with each unit. Where a tenant or owner has one of these as well as the flat itself, we can clear both together in a single visit — see Storage Unit Clearance for how that side of it works.

For Landlords and Letting Agents

If you're managing more than one property, a fast, predictable turnaround matters more than almost anything else — every day a unit sits uncleared is a day it's not earning rent. We can work to a fixed date, coordinate directly with a managing agent or caretaker for keys and access, and — for agents managing several units — handle clearances on an ongoing basis rather than a one-off quote each time. Get in touch to talk through a standing arrangement if that's useful.

For agents and property managers running a portfolio rather than a single unit, the recurring problem isn't any one clearance — it's the admin around repeating the same request every time a tenancy ends. A standing arrangement means we already know how you like to be briefed, who holds keys, and what "ready to re-list" looks like for your properties, so each new job starts from a shorter conversation rather than from scratch. That matters most when speed-to-relet is the priority: a flat that's cleared, checked, and photograph-ready a day or two after handback is a flat that isn't sitting empty on the market. Where a tenancy end date and a viewing date are close together, tell us both and we'll plan the clearance to fit the gap — our End of Tenancy Clearance page has more on how we handle that specific turnaround pressure.

What Affects the Cost

We don't quote apartment clearances off a flat rate, because the same volume of belongings can take very different amounts of time depending on the building. A free quote takes the following into account, and we're always upfront about which of these apply to your job before confirming a price:

  • Floor level. Ground floor and first floor are the quickest to clear; higher floors take longer, especially without a lift.
  • Lift vs stairs. A working goods lift is faster than a passenger lift, which is faster than stairs. If a lift is out of service on the day, that changes the job — tell us in advance if a building has a history of lift downtime.
  • Distance from parking to the unit. A van that can pull up outside the door is a different job to one that has to park on the street and carry everything through a lobby, along a corridor, and up in a lift.
  • Volume. A studio and a three-bed apartment clear at very different speeds, and volume is still the biggest single factor in any quote.
  • Access booking requirements. A building with a strict loading-bay window or a requirement for management company sign-off can add scheduling constraints, even if the clearance itself is straightforward.

We don't publish fixed prices because these factors genuinely change the job — but every quote is free, no-obligation, and built around the access details you give us, not a generic per-room rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need lift access booked in advance?

If the building requires it, yes — let us know the building's process and we'll factor the booking into scheduling, or coordinate directly with the management company if that's easier.

Can you clear an apartment on short notice?

Often, yes. Get in touch with your timeline and we'll confirm what's realistic.

Do you clear items a previous tenant left behind?

Yes — abandoned belongings are cleared the same as any other contents, sorted responsibly.

Can you work with our managing agent directly for access?

Yes, we regularly coordinate directly with agents and caretakers for keys, access windows, and building requirements.

Do you clear apartments with no lift?

Yes — stair-carrying is routine for us, whether that's a two-storey period conversion or a walk-up block with no lift at all. We plan the job around it rather than treating it as an obstacle, and it's one of the first things we ask about when scoping a quote.

Do you offer standing arrangements for letting agents with multiple units?

Yes — for agents and property managers turning over several units a year, we can set up an ongoing arrangement rather than starting from a fresh quote each time, which speeds up how quickly each unit gets scheduled.

Do you clear apartments for landlords between tenants?

Yes, this is one of the most common jobs we do — clearing a unit between one tenancy ending and the next beginning, including anything a previous tenant has left behind. See our End of Tenancy Clearance page for more on how we handle the turnaround specifically.

Can you coordinate with a management company for access?

Yes — where a building requires formal sign-off, insurance details, or a booked access window before a contractor can use common areas or a lift, we'll deal with that directly rather than leaving it to you to chase.

Do I need to be present on the day?

Not necessarily, provided we can get keys and clear access instructions in advance — many landlord and letting agent clearances are arranged this way, with the agent or caretaker handling access on the day.
