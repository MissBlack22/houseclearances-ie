Shed Clearance

A garden shed is where things go to be forgotten — old paint tins, tools that don't work anymore, a bike nobody's ridden since a previous decade, offcuts of timber "for a project," and whatever wouldn't fit in the house. Most sheds we clear haven't been properly sorted in years. HouseClearances.ie empties them out completely, disposes of what can't be reused properly, and gets the space back to something you can actually use.

What Typically Comes Out of a Shed

A shed clearout tends to sort itself into a handful of recognisable piles once you actually get everything out into the light. Knowing what's usually in there is part of quoting it accurately, so here's the breakdown of what we come across most:

  • Tools and DIY equipment — hand tools rusted solid, power tools that stopped working years ago, half-used boxes of screws and fittings, workbenches nobody's stood at since a previous house move.
  • Garden machinery — lawnmowers with seized engines, strimmers, hedge trimmers, old petrol cans, hosepipes tangled beyond untangling.
  • Bikes and sports gear — bicycles with flat tyres and rusted chains, kids' scooters long outgrown, camping gear, the odd surfboard or set of golf clubs that made it as far as the shed and no further.
  • Paint tins, chemicals, and hazardous-adjacent items — old paint, white spirit, weedkiller, engine oil. We flag these separately and route them through correct disposal channels rather than tipping them in with general waste.
  • Timber offcuts and building materials — leftover decking boards, fence panels, plasterboard, "useful bits" kept from a project that finished years ago.
  • General household overflow — boxes that never got unpacked, furniture that didn't fit inside, seasonal decorations, whatever got moved outside "temporarily" and stayed.

Some of this is genuinely awkward — heavy, rusted, half-buried under other items, or simply unpleasant to sort through after years of neglect. That's exactly the kind of job we're set up for.

Why This Job Is Different From a House Clearance

Sheds are typically weatherworn, cramped, and full of items that have been rained on, gone rusty, or partly rotted — different handling from clearing dry household contents. We come prepared for that, and we're used to working around gardens, gates, and narrow side-access rather than a front door.

Access is usually the deciding factor in how a shed job runs. A lot of Dublin gardens are reached through a narrow side passage, a locked gate, or a path that's more mud than paving by the time you get to the shed itself — nothing a front-door house clearance ever has to deal with. Some sheds have no proper flooring, so contents have sat directly on damp ground for years; others are packed so tightly that nothing can be lifted out until something else moves first. Rusted padlocks, swollen doors that won't open properly, and items that have partly rotted into the shed floor are all things we expect to find, not surprises that hold up the job. If access is particularly tight — no side gate, only a route through the house, or a shed at the end of a long uneven garden — telling us in advance means we can plan the right approach and the right vehicle before we arrive.

Responsible Disposal

We separate what can be reused or recycled from genuine waste, and route anything unsuitable through a licensed waste transfer station. Items still in decent condition — working tools, garden furniture — are set aside for donation or resale rather than binned automatically. We're a fully insured waste carrier, and aim for an average recycling rate of up to 95% across everything we clear.

When Shed Clearance Makes Sense

There's no single trigger for booking a shed clearout — it tends to come up around a few recurring situations:

  • General decluttering — the shed has become the place where everything gets stored "for now," and now has been going on for years.
  • Preparing a garden for sale or viewings — an empty, tidy shed reads a lot better than one stuffed with decades of overflow. This often runs alongside a wider garden waste clearance to get the whole outdoor space presentable.
  • A house move — sheds are usually the last thing anyone gets around to, and the first thing left behind. If you're clearing the whole property, we can quote the shed as part of a full house clearance rather than as a separate job.
  • Clearing the garage at the same time — shed and garage clearance are natural companions, since both tend to accumulate the same kind of overflow.
  • Inherited or estate properties — a garden shed is often one of the last spaces dealt with after a bereavement, and can be quietly overwhelming to sort through alone. Where that's part of a wider property clearance, our bereavement clearance service can cover it sensitively as part of the same visit.

What Affects the Cost

Every shed is quoted individually rather than at a flat rate, because the same size shed can mean very different jobs. The main factors are:

  • Volume — how full the shed actually is, not just its footprint. A small shed packed solid can take longer than a larger one that's mostly empty.
  • Access — a straightforward side gate versus a narrow passage, steps, or a route that has to go back through the house.
  • Hazardous-adjacent items — paint, chemicals, and similar items need to be handled and disposed of correctly, which factors into the quote.
  • Distance to the van — how far items have to be carried from the shed to where we can load up.

We don't publish a fixed price list because it wouldn't be honest — the fairest way is a free quote based on what's actually in your shed. Let us know roughly what's there and how it's accessed, and we'll give you a straight, no-obligation figure.
