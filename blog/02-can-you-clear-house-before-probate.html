Can You Clear a House Before Probate Is Granted?

This is a general guide, not legal advice — every estate is different, and if there's any doubt, a solicitor handling the probate can give a definitive answer for your specific situation.

Probate is a widely-used term, but not everyone understands exactly what it covers when it comes to clearing a deceased person's home. It's a common worry for families: can we start clearing the house, or do we need to wait for probate to be formally granted first?

What Probate Actually Covers

Probate is the legal process of confirming a will (or, where there's no will, appointing an administrator) and giving the executor or administrator the authority to deal with the estate — including selling property, closing accounts, and distributing assets. It's specifically about legal authority over the estate's affairs, not a blanket ban on anyone entering or tidying the property.

What's Generally Fine Before Probate Is Granted

  • Securing the property — making sure it's locked, insured, and safe.
  • Removing perishables — clearing the fridge, bins, anything that would otherwise create a problem.
  • Sorting personal effects that clearly have no bearing on the estate's value — photos, personal papers, everyday items.
  • General tidying that doesn't involve disposing of anything of significant value.

Where It's Worth Being Careful

The main thing to be cautious about is anything that could be considered part of the estate's assets — items of financial or sentimental value that other beneficiaries might have a claim to, or anything that could affect the valuation of the estate for probate purposes. If there's more than one beneficiary, or the will is contested or unclear, it's worth getting agreement from everyone involved (and ideally a nod from the solicitor) before a full clearance goes ahead.

A Practical Middle Ground

In practice, most families find a middle ground: securing the property and clearing anything urgent straight away, then arranging the fuller clearance once probate is granted or at least once things are clearer between beneficiaries. If you're not sure where your situation sits, a quick conversation with the solicitor handling the estate is worth having before anything is disposed of.
