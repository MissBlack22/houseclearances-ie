House Clearance in Portlaoise When You Can Only Be There on a Saturday

Portlaoise sits on the M7 and on the Dublin rail line, and like any commuter town it has plenty of houses whose owners live and work somewhere else. A parent's house, a house you moved out of and are now selling, a rental between tenants: whichever it is, you cannot take a week of leave to empty it, and you may not be able to take a single weekday. What you have is Saturdays, one or two of them, and the clearance has to fit into those.

That is a workable plan rather than a compromise, provided the Saturdays are used in the right order. We work Monday to Saturday, 8am to 5pm, and never on Sundays, so a Saturday clearance is an ordinary booking. This guide sets out how to spend the Saturday before, what the crew needs from you on the day, how to handle keys, alarms and locked rooms, and the weekday alternative where a neighbour holds the keys and you are not there at all. Two Saturdays, or One Saturday and a Weekday

There are three ways to arrange it, and the right one depends on how much is in the house and how much of the sorting you want to do yourself.

  • Two Saturdays. The first is yours: sorting, labelling, photographing, sending the photographs for a quote. The second is the clearance, with you there for the start and the finish. This is the simplest plan, and the rest of this guide follows it.
  • One Saturday and a weekday. You do the sorting on the Saturday. The clearance happens on a weekday while you are at work, with a neighbour or relative handing over the keys. You see the empty house in photographs that afternoon.
  • One Saturday only. Possible for a smaller house where little sorting is needed, with you sorting in the morning as the crew works through the rooms you have finished. It depends on the volume, and we will tell you from the photographs whether it is realistic rather than promising it.

The Saturday Before: Sort, Label, Photograph, Send

The first Saturday decides how the second one goes. Arrive early, bring boxes, bags, tape, a marker and a notebook, and work through the house in this order.

1. Find the things that must not leave

Before anything is sorted, go through drawers, presses, handbags, coat pockets, the bedside lockers and the usual hiding places for papers, keys, photographs, medication and any phone, tablet or laptop. These leave the house with you that evening. Our guide to old paperwork and documents during a clearance explains what to keep; we do not offer shredding, so sensitive papers are yours to deal with.

2. Walk every room with three categories

Keep, offer, go. Mark everything that is staying or going to a relative with coloured tape or a sticker, and move it into one room if you can. Make a list of what is being offered to family or a charity shop, with a photograph of each item, and send it round that evening with a deadline for answers. Everything without a label is going. Do not spend the day deciding about ornaments one at a time; put anything you are unsure about in a single box, labelled, and take it with you.

3. Photograph everything and send it

Take a short video of each room with wardrobe and press doors open, the attic and how you get up to it, the garage, the shed and the garden, the front of the house showing where a truck can stand, and any item that is heavy or awkward: a piano, a safe, a chest freezer, a wardrobe that does not come apart. Send it by WhatsApp with the area, the Saturday you have in mind and a note of what is staying. You get a fixed written price from the photographs; nothing is added on the day. The house clearance checklist is a useful thing to have open while you do this.

4. The practical jobs that only you can do

  • Empty the fridge and freezer and leave the doors open to defrost, if they are going.
  • Separate paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries and gas cylinders. We cannot take any of these. They go to a civic amenity site, or back to the supplier for gas cylinders, and that trip is easiest done by you on a Saturday with the car. See household versus hazardous waste.
  • Drain or run down the fuel in a lawnmower or strimmer before it travels.
  • Check the attic and the shed properly rather than glancing in. They are where the volume hides.
  • Tell the neighbours a truck will be outside on the date, and ask the one you trust to hold a key.

Between the Two Saturdays

The week in between is for the small jobs that need weekday phone calls. Confirm the date with us in writing. Chase the relatives for answers on the offered items, and arrange for them to collect before the clearance or to say clearly that they do not want them. Ring the charity shop before loading the car; shops set their own limits on furniture and space. If the house is being sold, check with the estate agent whether anything should be left for the photographs, and tell us.

Clearance Saturday: What the Crew Needs From You

The crew arrives at 8am, or at the time agreed. The day starts with a short walk-through: you show us the "keep" room, the labelled items, anything that has changed since the photographs, and where the truck is to stand. After that you have a choice. Some people stay for the whole day and find it useful; others leave once the work is under way and come back for the final walk-through in the afternoon. Both are fine. What the crew needs from you is clear labels, a decision on the "unsure" box before you leave, the water and electricity left on, and a phone number you will answer.

The crew clears room by room, larger items first, protects floors and door frames on the route out, and keeps items in good condition separate from waste so they can go for reuse where realistic. Garages and sheds are usually left until last, because they take longest to sort and hold most of what cannot travel with us. At the end there is a second walk-through, and the rooms are left clear and swept of loose debris. If you want anything photographed before it goes, say so during the first walk-through.

Keys, Alarms and Locked Rooms

Keys are the thing that goes wrong on a day with a fixed timetable, so sort them in advance.

  • Count the keys. Front and back doors, the garage, the shed, the side gate, the oil tank cap, the meter box. Label them.
  • Alarm codes. Give the code by phone or message to the person who needs it, on the day, and change it afterwards. Do not write it on a note left in the house.
  • Locked rooms. A room that is locked, or marked as out of scope, is not entered. If a room is locked by accident, the day loses time while someone finds the key, so check every door on the first Saturday.
  • Who gets the keys back. Decide before the day: you, the neighbour, the agent or the solicitor.

The Weekday Alternative: When a Neighbour Holds the Keys

If a second Saturday is not possible, the clearance can happen on a weekday without you. It works well when the first Saturday's sorting has been done properly, because the crew is then working from your labels rather than guessing. The sequence is this: the neighbour or relative hands over the key at the start, or lets the crew in; the crew confirms the scope with you by phone or video at the beginning; the labelled items and the "keep" room are not touched; anything personal that turns up unexpectedly, such as papers or photographs, is set aside and shown to you rather than removed; photographs of every empty room are sent to you that afternoon; and the keys go back to the neighbour or to whoever you have named. You see the result that afternoon and visit when you next can.

What Can Wait, and What Cannot

With limited days, it helps to be clear about what has to happen before the clearance and what can be dealt with afterwards from wherever you live.

  • Cannot wait: papers, valuables and photographs removed; hazardous items separated; the fridge emptied; the keys and alarm sorted; the labels on.
  • Can wait: deciding what to do with the keepsakes you took home; selling anything of value, which is better done without a deadline; cleaning, the garden and any repairs before a sale. Portlaoise Houses: What to Tell Us

Houses in and around Portlaoise fall into a few broad types. Older houses in and near the town centre tend to have tighter halls and stairs and less parking, which affects how furniture comes out. The estates built as the town grew along the M7 corridor usually have driveways, garages and attics, which means easy access and a lot stored out of sight. Houses on their own ground outside the town have sheds and outbuildings that should be walked and filmed as well. Tell us which you are dealing with, where a truck can stand, the distance to the door, and anything heavy; we do collect pianos.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you do house clearances in Portlaoise on Saturdays?

Yes. We work Monday to Saturday, 8am to 5pm, and not on Sundays. Tell us early if a particular Saturday is the only day you can be there.

Can the sorting and the clearance happen on the same Saturday?

Sometimes, in a smaller house where most of the sorting is already done. Send the photographs and we will say whether it is realistic. For a full family home, a separate sorting day beforehand works far better.

What should I do with paint, fuel and gas cylinders before the day?

Separate them on the first Saturday. We cannot take paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries or gas cylinders. Paint and chemicals go to a civic amenity site; gas cylinders go back to the supplier.

Can you clear the house on a weekday if a neighbour has the keys?

Yes. The crew collects the key, confirms the scope with you by phone or video, works from your labels, sets aside anything personal that turns up, sends photographs of the empty rooms that afternoon and returns the keys to whoever you name.

How do I give you the alarm code safely?

By phone or message to the crew on the day, not written down in the house. Change the code once the clearance is finished.

What if I am not sure about something on the sorting Saturday?

Put it in one labelled box and take it home with you. It is far easier to clear something later than to get it back once it has gone.

Is Portlaoise a long trip for you?

No. We are based in Athy, Co. Kildare, a short run from Portlaoise. Tell us the area when you send photographs and we will confirm the date.
