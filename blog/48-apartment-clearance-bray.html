Three Reasons an Apartment Gets Cleared, and Why the Ending Differs

The contents of a one-bedroom flat look much the same whoever owns them. The deadline, the person who decides what stays, and what the clearance has to prove at the end are different in each case.

The end of a tenancy

Either a tenant is leaving and wants the flat back to the state it was let in, or a landlord or letting agent has a flat that a tenant has already left, with belongings still in it. The deadline is the handover inspection or the next tenancy. What stays is everything that belongs to the landlord: furniture, appliances, curtains, fittings. What has to be proved at the end is the condition the flat was left in, because that is what any deposit discussion will turn on. Our guide to end of tenancy clearance and deposit deductions explains why belongings left behind cause more disputes than damage does, and the landlord clearance checklist covers the landlord's side. The service page is end of tenancy clearance.

Where a tenant has gone and left things behind, the practical steps for a landlord are to photograph everything as found, make a simple written inventory, and give the clearance company a written instruction about what is to be removed. That is practical advice, not legal advice: the rules on a former tenant's property are a matter for the Residential Tenancies Board and, where there is any doubt, a solicitor.

A sale

An owner is selling, or has already agreed a sale. The deadline is usually the estate agent's photographs first and the closing date second. What stays is whatever the contract says: fitted wardrobes, kitchen appliances, light fittings and curtains are often included, and the solicitor or agent can tell you exactly what was agreed. Do not remove anything that is in the contents list. What has to be proved at the end is simply that the apartment is empty and presentable on the day the agent, or the buyer, walks in.

An estate

The apartment belonged to someone who has died, and an executor or the family is clearing it. The deadline is set by the solicitor and by whether the flat is to be sold or let afterwards. Nothing in the apartment belongs to the building or a landlord, but the family will want papers, photographs and keepsakes found and set aside before anything else moves. What has to be proved at the end is a record for the executor: photographs of the empty rooms, confirmation of what was set aside, and the return of every key. See bereavement clearance for how we work with families and solicitors, and clearing a house before probate for the general position on timing. This is general guidance, not legal advice; the solicitor handling the estate decides what can go and when.

Who hands back the keys

In a tenancy, the tenant hands keys and fobs to the landlord or agent. In a sale, the seller hands them to the solicitor or agent for the buyer. In an estate, the executor hands them to the agent, the solicitor or the new owner. Decide at the start who that person is, and how many keys, fobs, gate remotes and post-box keys exist, because the clearance is not finished until they are all in the right hands.

What the Building Needs to Know First

Most apartment blocks are run by an owners' management company or a managing agent, and the shared parts of the building are their responsibility. Before you book a date, find out:

  • whether removals or clearances have to be notified or booked, and for which hours;
  • the size of the lift, and whether there is a separate service lift for bulky items;
  • whether a loading bay or set-down space can be reserved;
  • whether contractors must show insurance or a waste permit before working in common areas;
  • whether the bin store may be used at all. It usually may not be used for bulky items, and leaving furniture beside the bins is the quickest way to annoy the management company;
  • whether there is a basement store, bike store or locker that belongs to the unit.

Older buildings near the seafront and in the town centre, including houses divided into flats, may have no management company and no lift, just a narrow turning stairs. That is not a problem for the clearance, but it changes how furniture comes out and how long it takes, so tell us the floor and the number of flights. Parking a Truck in Bray

Pay parking covers much of Bray town centre and the seafront, and that is where many of the apartments are. The practical question is where a truck can stand for a few hours close enough to the door to be useful, because the distance from the truck to the lift decides how long the job takes. Where the block has a loading bay, a visitor space or a set-down area that can be reserved, ask the management company to book it for the day. Where it does not, tell us the street and we will plan the position of the truck and the loading route in advance rather than on the morning. Blocks on the estates further from the town centre are usually simpler. The Bray house clearance guide has more on parking in the town.

Sorting an Apartment: Less Volume, Fewer Places to Hide It

An apartment holds less than a house, but what it holds is harder to get out. A few points come up on nearly every apartment job.

  • The storage locker, the balcony and the bike store. They are left out of the photographs more often than any room, and they are where the surprises are. Include them in the quote.
  • White goods. Fridges and freezers need to be emptied and defrosted the day before. Washing machines and dishwashers need to be disconnected. Appliances go through electrical recycling rather than general waste; see what happens to old electronics during a clearance.
  • Mattresses and sofas. Bulky rather than heavy, and the items most likely to need dismantling to get around a stairwell.
  • Fitted items. Built-in wardrobes, the kitchen and anything screwed to the wall stay unless the landlord, the contract or the executor says otherwise.
  • Things we cannot take. Paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries and gas cylinders. Balconies and lockers often hold a few tins of paint and a camping gas cylinder; set them aside for a civic amenity site or the supplier. See household versus hazardous waste.

Protecting the Common Areas and the Neighbours

The people who notice an apartment clearance are the neighbours, and the management company hears from them. The crew protects the lift interior, hallway floors and door frames where the building requires it, props doors only while carrying, keeps the lift no longer than the booked window, and does not leave bags or furniture in corridors, stairwells or the bin store at any point. Everything goes straight from the flat to the truck. If the management company wants our insurance and waste permit details before the day, we provide them.

The Final Walk-Through and the Photographs

The clearance ends with a walk-through, and what it produces depends on who you are.

  • A tenant wants dated photographs of every room, cupboard and the balcony, empty, taken before the keys go back. They are the evidence if a deposit is questioned.
  • A landlord or letting agent wants the flat ready for the letting photographs and the next inspection: empty, swept of loose debris, with the landlord's own furniture left exactly where it was.
  • An executor wants a record for the estate file: photographs of the empty rooms, a note of what was set aside and handed to the family, and every key accounted for.

If you cannot be there, the crew does the walk-through on your behalf, sends the photographs the same afternoon, and hands the keys and fobs to whoever you have named. Anything personal that turns up during the clearance, such as papers or photographs, is set aside and shown to you rather than removed. Take meter readings while the flat is empty; they are easy to forget once the door is locked.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who books the lift or the loading bay, you or us?

It is usually simplest if the booking comes from the owner, tenant or agent, since the management company already knows them. Make it as soon as you have a date and tell us the window. If the management company is happy to deal with us directly, we will arrange it.

Where will the truck park for an apartment near the seafront or in Bray town centre?

Wherever the building allows and the street permits: a loading bay, a reserved visitor space or a set-down area if there is one. Tell us the street when you send photographs and we plan the position and the carrying route in advance.

Do fitted wardrobes, the kitchen and the appliances stay or go?

They stay unless the landlord, the sale contract or the executor says otherwise. In a rental they belong to the landlord; in a sale, check the contents list with your solicitor or agent before removing anything.

Can you clear an apartment between the agent's photographs and the closing date?

Yes. Tell us both dates and which pieces must stay for the photographs. The clearance is fitted between them, with a margin before closing.

What does the final walk-through involve if I cannot be there?

The crew checks every room, cupboard, the balcony and any locker, photographs them empty, sends you the photographs the same afternoon, and hands the keys and fobs to whoever you have named.

Who gets the keys and fobs when the clearance is finished?

Whoever you tell us: the landlord, the letting agent, the estate agent, the solicitor or a family member. Count the keys, fobs, gate remotes and post-box keys before the day so that none is missed.

Can you work within the building's hours for removals?

Yes. Many blocks allow removals only in set hours or on certain days. Tell us the window when you book and the crew arrives and finishes inside it. We work Monday to Saturday, 8am to 5pm, and not on Sundays.
