Apartment Clearance in Newbridge Is Not a City Job

Guides to apartment clearance tend to assume a big managed building with a concierge, a goods lift and a loading bay. Our own guide to apartment clearance in Bray deals with blocks like that, and the apartment clearance service page covers the work in general.

Newbridge is different. Like most county towns, much of its apartment stock is small: a flat over a shop on or near the main street, the upper floors of an older house, or a small block around one stairwell. There is rarely a lift, and often there is no management company to notify, only a landlord or an owner. That makes some things simpler and others harder, and both are worth knowing before you ask for a price.

Three Kinds of Apartment, One Staircase Each

The flat over a shop. The street door is between two shopfronts, or around the back off a lane or yard. The stairs are usually steep and straight, with a tight turn at the top, and they were not built with a sofa in mind.

The converted house. Two or three flats share the original hall and stairs. Other tenants use the same stairs all day, so the work has to leave them room.

The small block. A purpose-built stairwell with landings, a few parking spaces and a bin area. This is the easiest of the three, and still has no lift.

Tell us which one you have and send a photo of the stairs from the bottom. It changes how we plan the day more than the number of rooms does.

Loading on a Busy Street

With a flat over a shop, the hardest part of the job is often the ten paces between the street door and the truck. We will not guess at the parking and loading rules outside your building, because they differ from one stretch of street to the next. Two things help us plan it properly:

  • A photo of the street outside the door, including any signs on the nearest pole.
  • A photo of the back, if there is a rear lane, a yard or a car park the truck could use instead.

Where the truck cannot stay at the door, we bring everything down to the hall first and load in short, full runs. An 8am start is usually the quietest time on a main street, and we keep the footpath and the neighbouring shop doors clear while we work.

When There Is No Management Company

In a small building, the person to tell is the landlord or the owner, and after that the neighbours. A message a few days ahead with the date and the time is enough. It is worth asking the landlord three things in the same message:

  • Which items in the flat are theirs and must stay.
  • Whether the rear lane, yard or gate can be used on the day, and who has the key.
  • Where the photos of the empty rooms should be sent.

If there is a shop downstairs, a word with them the day before is good manners and practical too. They know when their own deliveries arrive, and it is better that our truck and theirs are not at the door at the same moment.

One rule holds everywhere: the bins at the back, whether they belong to the flats or to the shop, are not for mattresses, chairs or broken furniture. Bulky items go in our truck.

Frequently Asked Questions

The flat is on the second floor over a shop with no lift. Can you still clear it?

Yes. Everything is carried down by our crew, with beds and wardrobes taken apart where the stairs need it. Send a photo of the stairs from the bottom and tell us the floor, and the price will already allow for it.

Where will the truck park?

That depends on your street, so we ask for a photo of the street door and any signs nearby, and of the back of the building if there is a lane or yard. Where the truck cannot wait at the door, we bring items down first and load in short runs.

I am a tenant. Can you take only my own furniture?

Yes. Show us which pieces are yours in the photos and mark the landlord's items if it is not obvious. We take only what you have shown us and leave the rest in place.

Can you send photos of the empty flat to my landlord?

Yes. Each room is photographed once it is empty and swept, and we can send the photos to you, to the landlord or to an agent, whichever you ask for.

The sofa will not go down the stairs in one piece. What happens?

Many sofas come apart further than people expect: feet, arms and sometimes the back unbolt. Send a photo of the sofa and one of the turn in the stairs. If it cannot come down whole, we will tell you beforehand how we plan to deal with it.

Do I need to be there?

No. We can collect keys from you, the landlord or an agent, and we send photos when the flat is empty. If someone is holding keys for you, tell us who and when they are available.

What do you not take?

Paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries, gas cylinders, asbestos and clinical waste. Anything that belongs to the building, such as fitted wardrobes, blinds or the landlord's own appliances, stays unless the owner asks for it to go.

Do you work on Saturdays?

Yes. We work Monday to Saturday from 8am to 5pm, and not on Sundays. An early start is often the best time for a flat on a main street.
