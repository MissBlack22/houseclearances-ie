Storage Unit Clearance in Killiney

Plenty of families are spread across more than one country. The house on the hill is sold after a parent dies or moves, the sons and daughters fly in for a fortnight, and there is far too much to decide in that time. A storage unit buys the family a pause. Everyone goes back to their own lives, and the pause becomes permanent.

Our storage unit clearance page describes the service in general, and our page on house clearance in Killiney covers the area. This page is for the person whose name is on the account and who is not in Ireland to deal with it.

What Can Be Done Without You

Nearly all of it. A storage unit is one of the easier jobs to hand over from abroad, because nobody lives there and nothing has to be lived around. Two things make it possible.

  • The facility agrees to let us in. That is between you, as the account holder, and them. Each facility handles it in its own way, so ask what they need from you, and make sure the access and authorisation are in place before the day.
  • You and we agree what is going. In advance, from photos and a list, and again at the door of the unit before anything is moved.

What we need from you is short: the name of the facility, the unit size, how access works, and what, if anything, is being kept.

Three Things to Settle From Where You Are

None of them needs a flight. All of them are easier done before a date is set.

What Is Actually in There

You may not remember, and that is common. Look for the photos taken on the day the unit was filled. Ask the brother or sister who was there. If nobody can say, we can still quote from the unit size and clear it without you going through everything first.

If there is something you would be sorry to lose, tell us what it is and what it looks like. A description of one box is more use than a guess at the whole unit.

Who Stands In for You

It helps to have one person in Ireland who can be at the unit for the first half hour: a brother or sister, a cousin, an old neighbour from the road. They are not there to carry anything. They are there to take the few things that are being kept, and to be a second pair of eyes.

If there is nobody, the job can still go ahead. The things you are keeping are set to one side in the unit, and you decide later who collects them.

What You Are Keeping

Be strict with yourself here. Anything kept has to go somewhere, and from abroad that means a relative's house or a parcel. Agree a limit that can be posted or minded, such as one box of photographs and papers, and let the furniture go.

We do not pack or ship abroad. What you keep is handed to the person standing in for you, or left to one side in the unit for them to collect.

How the Day Is Run When You Are Not There

  • We arrive inside the facility's hours and go through whatever sign-in they ask for.
  • The scope is confirmed with you by video at the open door of the unit, so you see what is there before anything is touched. Tell us your time zone when you book, and we agree a time that is not the middle of your night.
  • Kept things come out first and are handed over or set to one side.
  • Boxes are looked into as they are carried out. Personal papers and photographs that turn up are set aside and shown to you, not loaded.
  • The unit is left empty and photographed. You get the photos as soon as the unit is empty.

After that, closing the account is a matter between you and the facility. Ask them in advance what they need to see, so that our photos of the empty unit are enough for you to finish it from abroad.

If You Are Coming Home Anyway

Some people would sooner be there, and a visit home for a wedding or a holiday is a chance to do it. Two things make that work.

Worth doing

  • Book the clearance for the first days of the trip, not the last
  • Get the quote and the facility's agreement before you travel
  • Ask the family to say what they want before you land
  • Bring one empty suitcase for what you are taking back

Worth avoiding

  • Leaving it to the morning of the flight back
  • Planning to sort every box yourself in one afternoon
  • Moving the keepers to another relative's attic for later
  • Keeping the unit open for the sake of one piece of furniture

At the Storage Facility

Storage facilities have their own access rules, and no two run things in quite the same way. Some are open long hours, and others keep to a set window and expect notice when a crew and a truck are coming. Loading bays often have to be booked, and a unit on an upper floor means a lift or a trolley run. Tell us which facility it is and what access looks like, and we work to their hours.

If the account is in the name of a parent who has died, or of an estate, the facility will have its own requirements before it lets anyone in. Settle that with them first. Our bereavement clearance page explains how we work with families in that position.

What We Take and What We Cannot

We take household furniture, beds and mattresses, boxes of books and kitchenware, pictures, rugs, lamps, appliances and whatever else came out of the house. Pieces in good condition are set aside for reuse and donation where possible. We cannot take paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries, gas cylinders, asbestos or clinical waste. If any of those turn up in the unit, we stand them to one side and tell you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the unit really be cleared without me being there?

Yes, as long as the facility's access arrangements allow it and we have agreed in advance what is being kept and what is being cleared. Our crew confirms the scope with you by video at the start and sends photos when the unit is empty.

What does the storage facility need from me?

That depends on the facility, so ask them directly. As the account holder you need to have the access and authorisation in place for us before the day. Tell us what they have asked for and we bring whatever is needed.

I cannot remember what is in the unit. Can you still quote?

Yes. The unit size, which is usually on your account or contract, and a rough description are normally enough. If you have old photos from the day it was filled, send those as well.

Can you send the things I want to keep to me abroad?

No. We do not pack or ship abroad. What you are keeping is handed to a relative or friend at the unit, or left to one side in the unit for them to collect.

What if you find something personal that I did not know was there?

Personal papers and photographs are set aside as we find them and shown to you, not loaded. If we are unsure about anything else, we ask you before it goes.

How do I close the account afterwards?

That is between you and the facility. Ask them beforehand what they need. We send you photos of the empty unit when we finish, and you can pass those on to them.

The time difference is large. When does the video call happen?

We work Monday to Saturday, 8am to 5pm Irish time. Tell us where you are when you book, and we pick a start time inside those hours that falls at a reasonable hour for you.

There is more than one unit. Can they be done together?

Yes. Tell us how many units there are and where they are, and we quote for the whole job together.
