House Clearance in Portarlington

Portarlington sits on the River Barrow where Laois meets Offaly, with a railway station that puts it within reach of Dublin for family members coming down for the day. Alongside the older streets in the town and the newer estates, many of the homes on the roads out of town are bungalows on their own sites, often lived in by one family for decades.

When one of those houses has to be emptied, after a death, a move into care or a sale, the work is not quite what people expect. Our house clearance page describes the service in general. This page is about the bungalow in particular, because it clears differently from a two-storey house in three ways that matter.

Why a Bungalow Clears Differently

  • The rooms are quick. Everything is on one level, the hall is usually wide and the truck can often reverse to the door. Furniture that is slow work on a staircase goes straight out the door.
  • The attic is big. It runs over the whole footprint of the house, not half of it, and in a home that was lived in for decades it has usually been filled from the hatch outwards.
  • There is more outside. A garage, a boiler house, a fuel shed, a garden shed and sometimes a workshop. On a site with room to build, people built.

The practical result is that the photos of the attic and the outbuildings tell us more about the size of the job than the photos of the bedrooms. If you can only send a few, send those.

Every Part of the Property, Not Just the Rooms

These are the three places that decide how long a bungalow takes, and what to look for in each before you ask for a price.

The Attic

Open the hatch, stand on the ladder and take a photo in each direction. Tell us whether there is a fixed attic stairs or a loose ladder, and whether the floor is boarded. Everything comes down through the hatch by hand, so boxes, suitcases, Christmas decorations and rolled carpet are straightforward, and old water tanks and anything built in up there are not.

Insulation stays, as it is part of the house. Our guide to what is worth keeping from an attic is useful before the day, and attic clearance can be booked on its own.

The Garage

A garage attached to a bungalow has usually stopped holding a car a long time ago. Expect tools, timber, a chest freezer, bicycles, a lawnmower and the furniture that was replaced indoors. All of that goes. Paint, oils, fuel and gas cylinders do not: we cannot take them, so we stand them together in one corner and tell you where they are.

See garage clearance for how a garage is cleared on its own.

The Sheds and the Fuel Store

Garden sheds, a turf or coal shed and a boiler house are all cleared as part of the same job. Leftover turf, coal and timber can stay for whoever is taking the house if you would like them to, so tell us. An oil tank and whatever is in it stay too: heating oil is not something we can take, and an oil supplier can advise on having a tank emptied.

See shed clearance, and garden waste clearance if the site itself needs cutting back before a sale.

What Stays With the House

A clearance takes the contents, not the building. In a bungalow that line is worth agreeing in advance, because a lot of what is in one was fitted by the owner.

We take

  • Furniture, beds, mattresses and wardrobes that are not built in
  • Carpets and rugs that are to go
  • Cookers, fridges, freezers and washing machines
  • Kitchen contents, clothes, books and ornaments
  • Everything in the attic, garage and sheds
  • Garden furniture, pots and tools

Stays unless you say otherwise

  • The range or stove and its flue
  • The oil tank and boiler
  • Fitted kitchens and built-in wardrobes
  • Curtain poles, blinds and light fittings
  • Attic insulation and water tanks
  • Anything the estate agent has listed as included

If the house is being sold, ask the estate agent or solicitor what the buyer has been told is included, and send us that list with the photos.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you cover Portarlington on both the Laois and the Offaly side?

Yes. The county boundary makes no difference to us. Send the address or Eircode with your photos and we will confirm the date.

Is a bungalow cheaper to clear than a two-storey house?

The rooms are quicker because there are no stairs, but a bungalow often has a bigger attic and more outbuildings, so the total can be much the same. The price depends on the size of the load, the access and what the load is made of, which is why we ask for photos of the attic, garage and sheds as well as the rooms.

The attic has only a hatch and a loose ladder. Can you still clear it?

Yes. Tell us the size of the hatch and whether the floor is boarded. Everything is handed down by our crew. If the attic is not boarded, tell us, because it affects how the job is planned.

Can you take the oil in the tank, or bags of coal and turf?

We cannot take heating oil or any other fuel. An oil supplier can advise on emptying a tank. Coal, turf and firewood are often left for the next owner; if you want them gone, ask us when you send photos and we will tell you what we can do.

Will you remove the range or the stove?

Not as part of a clearance. A range or stove is fitted to the house and its flue, so it stays unless a builder or installer is taking it out.

Nobody in the family lives nearby. How do we get you in?

Leave keys with a neighbour, the solicitor or the estate agent and send us their number. We collect the keys, clear the house, return them and send you photos of every room.

Can the garden be cleared at the same time?

Yes. Garden furniture, pots, old fencing and green waste can be taken on the same job. Send a photo of the front and back so we can include it in the price.

What happens to things you find that look personal?

Personal papers, photographs and keepsakes are set aside in one place and shown to the family. They are not put in the truck.
