Why an Attic Cannot Be Sorted Where It Is

  • You cannot see. One bulb, if there is one, and a torch for the corners.
  • You cannot stand. Under the eaves you are crouching, and where there are no boards you are balancing on joists.
  • You cannot spread anything out. A box of photographs needs a table, not a knee.
  • You cannot stay long. It is cold in winter, hot in summer and dusty all year.
  • Every box has to be moved twice, once to get at the one behind it and once to put it back.

Most people who start sorting an attic from the ladder give up after an hour, and the attic stays as it was for another year. The fix is not more willpower. It is a different order of work.

Three Stages, in This Order

The lifting is ours. The deciding is yours, and it happens sitting down.

Stage One: Everything Comes Down

You choose a sorting room: a dining room, a spare bedroom or the garage. Our crew protects the landing and the stairs, then brings the whole attic down through the hatch and carries it to that room. Boxes that have gone soft are repacked first so they do not burst on the ladder.

Things that are plainly finished, such as broken furniture, empty boxes and old carpet, can go straight to the truck on the first day if you tell us to. Everything else waits for you.

Stage Two: The Family Sorts, Sitting Down

We set things out by type, not in the order they came down: papers together, photographs and albums together, decorations, children's things, books, furniture. That way each kind of decision is made once.

Take a weekend or take a month. Use three labels, keep, give and go, and put a name on anything a family member has claimed. Our guide to what is worth keeping from an attic helps with the boxes nobody is sure about.

Stage Three: We Come Back for the Rest

When you have finished, tell us. We return, carry out everything that is not marked to keep and leave the room swept. If you want the kept boxes put back up in the attic, neatly and near the hatch, we do that on the same visit.

It is one job with one price, agreed before the first visit, so there is nothing to renegotiate in the middle.

One Day or Two Visits?

Not every attic needs two visits. A smaller one can be brought down and checked box by box in a single visit, with you looking at each thing before it goes to the truck. The choice comes down to how much there is and how many people need to see it.

One day suits

  • A modest attic, mostly boxes and decorations
  • One person making the decisions
  • A family that already knows what it wants
  • A house that has to be empty by a set date

Two visits suit

  • A large attic filled over several decades
  • Brothers and sisters who each want a look
  • Boxes of papers and photographs to read through
  • A parent who wants to take their time

Tell us which you want when you send photos. Bringing everything down for you to check takes longer than clearing straight to the truck, so it is part of the quote from the start.

What Stays in the Attic

A clearance takes what was stored, not what was built. Insulation stays, as it is part of the house. So do the water tanks, the pipework and any wiring. Loose boards and offcuts lying about can go if you want them gone. If we see anything that looks like it may contain asbestos, such as old pipe lagging, we leave it alone and tell you, because that needs a specialist contractor.

Getting It Down Safely

How the attic is reached shapes the first visit. A fixed stairs and a boarded floor is close to clearing an ordinary room. A fold-away ladder means everything comes down one item at a time. A plain hatch with no ladder means a stepladder on the landing and everything passed hand to hand.

Where there is no floor, our crew works from the joists and keeps its weight off the ceiling below. Furniture that will not fit through the opening is taken apart in the attic. None of this is a problem, but all of it takes time, so tell us what you have.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does it cost more to have everything brought down for sorting?

It takes longer than clearing straight to the truck, so it is priced for the extra time. Tell us in advance that you want to go through things and the quote covers both visits.

How long can we take to sort?

That is up to you. Tell us roughly when you expect to be finished and we book the second visit for after that. If you need longer, let us know and we move it.

Which room is best for sorting?

One with a door you can close, room for a table and daylight. A dining room or a spare bedroom works well. The garage is fine in summer, but paper and photographs should not be left there for long.

Can you put the kept boxes back up in the attic?

Yes. Mark them and we put them back on the second visit, near the hatch and on the boards, so they are easy to reach.

The attic has no floor. Can you still clear it?

Yes. Our crew works from the joists and keeps its weight off the plasterboard between them. Tell us it is not boarded, because it affects how long the first visit takes.

There is only a hatch and a loose ladder. Is that a problem?

No. It is one of the most common setups we meet. Everything is passed down by hand, which is slower than a fixed stairs, and we allow for that in the quote.

Do you take the insulation or the water tank?

No. Insulation, tanks, pipes and wiring are part of the house and stay where they are. We clear what was stored in the attic.

What if you find papers or photographs we did not know were there?

They are kept together and put where you will see them first. With this way of working nothing leaves the house until the family has been through it.
