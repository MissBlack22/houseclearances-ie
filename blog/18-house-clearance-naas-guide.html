House Clearance in Naas: A Local Guide

Naas has grown fast over the last two decades, and that shows up in the mix of properties we clear there — period houses close to the town centre, large 1990s and 2000s estates on the outskirts, and a steady stream of apartment developments near the M7 and the town's retail parks. Each type comes with its own practicalities, and a bit of planning before the day makes the clearance itself much smoother. Here's what's usually worth knowing if you're arranging a house clearance in Naas or elsewhere in County Kildare.

Property Types and What They Mean for the Job

Older terraced and semi-detached houses near the town centre, around streets like the Sallins Road or South Main Street, often have limited off-street parking and narrower hallways, which can add time to carrying larger furniture out. Estates further out — around Craddockstown Road, towards Two Mile House, or the newer developments off the Kilcullen Road — generally have driveways and easier van access. Apartments are the ones that most need advance notice: lift size, whether there's a designated loading bay, and whether the management company needs to be told all affect how a job is scheduled. None of this changes whether we can do the work, but it does feed into how long it takes and what we quote.

Parking Around Naas Town Centre

Naas town centre operates pay parking, managed under contract for Kildare County Council, with the town divided into separate zones and tariffs — shorter maximum stays closer to the centre and longer ones further out, plus a short observation period before a ticket is required. If your property is inside one of the paid zones, it's worth thinking about where a clearance van can realistically sit for a few hours without racking up parking charges or blocking a restricted space. Mention your street when you get in touch and we'll factor it into how we plan the day — it's a common question and usually easy to work around.

Where Cleared Items Actually Go

We sort everything as we go, aiming for donation and recycling ahead of disposal wherever an item is in reusable condition. Naas has a few options for furniture donation specifically: Vincent's Furniture Shop, run by the Society of St. Vincent de Paul on Friary Road, takes donated furniture as well as clothing, and Renaissance, a charity shop on the Sallins Road, also stocks furniture alongside bric-a-brac and homeware. Charity shops set their own rules on what they can take and how much space they have on a given week, so it's always worth a phone call rather than assuming.

For recycling and civic amenity drop-off, the main facility serving Naas is a privately run recycling centre at Osberstown Business Park, which accepts household waste categories including electricals and batteries. Kildare County Council's own council-run recycling centres, operated on its behalf, are based in Kilcullen and Athy rather than in Naas itself — worth knowing if you're planning to bring something there yourself rather than having it collected as part of a clearance.

Why There's No Council Bulky Collection to Book

As across the rest of Ireland, household waste collection — including bulky items like furniture and appliances — is handled by private operators rather than the local authority. There's no Kildare County Council bulky waste collection you can simply book for a house clearance. Instead, you're hiring a waste collector that holds a valid waste collection permit for the region.

That permit is the thing worth checking whoever you use, since it's what confirms items are actually going somewhere legitimate rather than being fly-tipped, which can land back on the person who handed the goods over. Timing and What Helps Before We Arrive

Most single-property clearances in Naas are completed within a day; larger houses, or ones with a garage and attic on top of the main rooms, sometimes run a little longer. If you're working to a sale completion date, a rental handover, or another fixed deadline, tell us early and we'll give you a straight answer on whether it's achievable.

The most useful thing you can do beforehand is set aside anything you're definitely keeping in one room or with a clear label, so nothing gets swept up by mistake.
