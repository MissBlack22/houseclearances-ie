Preparing a Rental Property for New Tenants: A Landlord's Checklist

The gap between one tenant moving out and the next moving in is often tighter than landlords would like, and it's easy for the clearance and prep work to get rushed. A clear checklist helps make sure nothing gets missed — and nothing gets left behind for the new tenant to discover.

Start With What's Actually Being Left Behind

Before anything else, walk the property and note what the outgoing tenant has left — furniture, appliances, rubbish, or anything abandoned in a rush. Under Irish tenancy rules, there are proper steps for dealing with a former tenant's belongings before disposing of them, so it's worth checking your obligations rather than clearing everything immediately.

Check the Spaces Tenants Often Forget

The main rooms usually get a quick check, but attics, sheds, garages and under-stairs storage are where things genuinely get left behind — old furniture, boxes, broken appliances, sometimes years of accumulated clutter from tenant to tenant. These spaces take real time to clear properly and are the ones most likely to be rushed or skipped entirely.

Separate Clearance From Cleaning

It's tempting to have one team do both, but clearance and deep cleaning are different jobs done in the wrong order if combined. A property needs to be fully cleared — furniture, rubbish, leftover belongings — before a proper end-of-tenancy clean can actually reach the floors, skirting boards and surfaces underneath everything.

Deal With Damage and Repairs While It's Empty

An empty property between tenancies is the easiest time to spot and fix damage — a stained carpet, a damaged door, marks on walls — without working around anyone's belongings or schedule. Clearing the property fully first makes it much easier to see what actually needs attention versus what's simply cluttered.

Photograph the Property Once It's Cleared

Once the property is cleared, cleaned and any repairs are done, photograph every room properly. This protects you at the end of the next tenancy and gives incoming tenants a clear record of the property's condition from day one — which cuts down on disputes later.
