Hoarder Clearance in Carlow, Done With the Person and Not to Them

Most of what is written about hoarder clearance describes an empty house at the end: every room stripped, the keys handed over. That is one kind of job, and our hoarder clearance service page and the longer guide to hoarder clearance cover it in detail.

This page is about the other kind, where someone is still living in the house and intends to stay. It might be a parent in a terraced house in Carlow Town, an uncle in a bungalow on the road out to Tullow, or a neighbour in Bagenalstown who has finally asked for help. Nobody is selling anything. The aim is a home that is safe and comfortable to live in again, and the only way that lasts is if the person agrees to each step.

We are a clearance company. We do not diagnose anything and this page is not medical advice. If you are worried about someone's health, their GP or public health nurse is the right person to talk to. What we can do is the practical part, carefully.

Start With Agreement, Not With a Skip

A skip on the road and a family arriving with bin bags is the approach most likely to end the conversation for good. A clear-out that was not agreed rarely lasts, and it costs trust that is hard to win back. A slower start usually gets further.

  • Ask what bothers them, not what bothers you. It may be the cold kitchen, not being able to use the bath, or having nowhere to sit. Start there.
  • Talk about rooms and uses, not about "the mess". "Would it help to be able to cook again?" is a question people can say yes to.
  • Agree one area. One hallway is a decision. A whole house is a threat.
  • Promise that nothing goes without a yes, and keep the promise, even when it is frustrating.
  • Do not clear while they are out. It works once and makes every later visit harder.

If the answer is still no, that is the answer for now. We would rather be asked back in three months than turned away at the door.

What a First Clearance Usually Covers

The first visit is not about emptying anything. It is about making the house work again: a clear way in and out, a kitchen that can be used, a bathroom that can be reached and a bed that can be slept in.

Usually agreed first

  • The hall, the stairs and the landing
  • The route to the front and back doors
  • The cooker, the sink and one worktop
  • The bath or shower and the toilet
  • The bed and the way to it
  • Obvious rubbish: packaging, old newspapers, spoiled food, broken appliances

Left alone until asked

  • Collections of any kind
  • Books, records, tools and hobby materials
  • Boxes of papers and photographs
  • Wardrobes and drawers
  • Spare rooms, the attic and the sheds
  • Anything the person hesitates over

A first clearance on those terms is the visit that makes a second one possible. Our guide to full and partial house clearances explains how a part clearance is planned and priced.

What We Look Out For as We Work

In a very full house, important things are rarely where you would expect. Post is in carrier bags, documents are between newspapers and keys are in coat pockets. Our crew works by hand, not by the armful, and anything personal is put into a box that stays in the house.

  • Letters, bills and anything with a name, an account number or an official heading
  • Photographs, cards and family papers
  • Keys, glasses, hearing aids and mobile phones
  • Medication, which is left where the person can find it and never thrown out by us
  • Anything the person has asked us to look out for

At the end of the day the box is gone through with the family. If you already know something is missing in the house, tell us before we start and we will look for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you clear just a few rooms and leave the rest?

Yes. A first clearance is nearly always partial: the hall and stairs, the kitchen, the bathroom and a bedroom. Everything outside the agreed list is left exactly as it is, and you can add to it at a later visit or never.

Does the person who lives there have to be present?

It is their choice. Some people want to see each thing go and others would sooner be out with a family member. What matters is that they have agreed the list beforehand. We do not clear a lived-in home behind the back of the person who lives in it.

How do you price a house when the rooms cannot be seen properly?

From photos taken at each doorway, plus what you can tell us about how deep the rooms are filled. If that is not enough to judge, we arrange a quick look first. The price is then fixed in writing for the agreed work.

Will the neighbours know what is happening?

They will see a van and a crew carrying things out, as they would for any house move. We do not discuss the job with neighbours or passers-by, and we load as quickly as the house allows so the van is not there longer than it needs to be.

What happens if they change their mind on the day?

Then that item, or that room, stays. Anything can come off the list while we are there. Once something has left in the van it cannot be brought back, so if there is any doubt we leave it.

Do you clean the house afterwards?

Cleared areas are left empty and swept. We are a clearance company, so a full professional clean is a separate service that you would arrange once the rooms are clear.

What can you not take away?

Paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries, gas cylinders, asbestos and clinical waste. We put those to one side, tell you where they are and explain where they can go.

How far into County Carlow do you travel?

All of it. Our base is in Athy, so Carlow Town, Tullow, Bagenalstown, Leighlinbridge and the villages between them are close to home for us. Send the address or Eircode with your photos.
