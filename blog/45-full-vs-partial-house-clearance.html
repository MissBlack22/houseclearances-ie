Full vs Partial House Clearance: What's the Difference?

"House clearance" gets used as a catch-all term, but the jobs it covers vary enormously. Emptying an entire three-bedroom house after a death in the family is a house clearance. So is clearing out one spare room before a new baby arrives. The two jobs need very different planning, take very different amounts of time, and are quoted differently. Here's what actually separates a full clearance from a partial one, and how to work out which one fits your situation.

What a Full House Clearance Covers

A full clearance means every room in the property is emptied: bedrooms, living areas, kitchen, bathroom, plus anywhere else that holds belongings — hallway presses, attic, garage, shed and garden. Nothing is left behind except what's part of the fabric of the house, such as fitted kitchen units or carpets that are staying. This is the kind of clearance that happens when a property is being sold, when a tenancy is ending and the home needs to be handed back empty, or after a death when the whole house needs to be cleared for probate or sale. Because every room is in scope, it's also the easiest type to quote accurately from photos or a walkthrough — there's no ambiguity about what's included.

What a Partial Clearance Covers

A partial clearance covers only the areas or items that have been agreed in advance, and everything else in the property is left untouched. Common examples are a single overstuffed room, a garage or shed that's become a dumping ground, an attic that needs sorting before a renovation, or a specific piece of furniture that won't fit in a new home. Partial clearances are the right fit when people are still living in the property, when only part of a house is being renovated or sold, or when a family is downsizing and only some rooms' worth of belongings need to go — see our guide to what to keep, sell, donate or clear when downsizing for how that sorting usually works in practice.

Why the Distinction Matters for a Quote

Being specific about scope matters because the two jobs are priced on different things. A full clearance is quoted on the whole property, so photos of every room — including attic, garage and shed — give the most accurate figure. A partial clearance is quoted only on the rooms or items named, and it helps to be clear about what is not included. If your request is "clear the house" but you actually mean "clear the garage and the back bedroom," saying so up front avoids a wasted site visit and a quote that doesn't match what you need done.

How the Day Itself Differs

A full clearance with an attic, garage and a few sheds is usually a longer job simply because there's more ground to cover. A partial clearance is often quicker precisely because it's contained — the crew knows exactly which rooms are in play. The trade-off is that a partial clearance usually needs more care, since furniture and belongings that are staying have to be worked around rather than simply carried out.

When the Two Overlap

Plenty of real jobs sit between the two categories. A house being sold after probate might need a full clearance of most rooms but leave a garden shed for the new owner. A family moving a parent into care might clear the whole house except for one room kept for storage. Rather than trying to force a job into "full" or "partial," the more useful approach is listing room by room what's going and what's staying — that's what actually drives both the quote and the plan for the day.

A Few Practical Points

  • Say what's staying, not just what's going. For a partial clearance, naming the rooms or items that should not be touched is just as useful as naming what should.
  • Photograph every room for a full clearance quote, including attic, garage and shed, so nothing is missed and added on the day.
  • A job can change scope partway through. If a "clear the garage" job turns up more in the shed too, tell the crew — they can usually adjust on the spot.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a partial clearance cheaper than a full clearance?

Not automatically — it depends on the volume and type of items involved, not just the number of rooms. A single room packed with heavy furniture can take as much effort as several lighter ones. The only way to know is to get a quote based on what's actually there.

Can I upgrade a partial clearance to a full one on the day?

Often, yes, if the crew has capacity in the van and the schedule that day. It's better to mention the possibility when booking rather than raising it for the first time once the crew has arrived.

Do you clear gardens, sheds and attics as part of a partial clearance?

Yes, if they're named as part of the job. A partial clearance can be any combination of rooms or outdoor spaces — it just means some parts of the property are excluded, not that outdoor areas are automatically out of scope.
