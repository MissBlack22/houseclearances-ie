What a Quote Is Built From

A clearance price comes down to a few things: how much is in the house, including the attic and outbuildings; how heavy it is, because a room of books weighs far more than a room of clothes; how awkward it is to get out, meaning stairs, turns, distance to the truck and anything that must be dismantled; what is staying; and whether anything needs special handling. A photograph answers each question if it is taken with the question in mind. A visit answers them too, but costs you a day's travel. Our guide to what drives house clearance costs goes through the factors in more detail.

Every Room From the Doorway

Stand in the doorway of each room and take one photograph that gets the whole room in, or a slow video pan from one side to the other. That picture tells us the size of the room, the furniture in it and how full it is. Then take two or three more: open every wardrobe, press and drawer bank and photograph the inside, look under the bed, and photograph anything stacked behind a door or in a corner. In the kitchen, open the presses, the fridge and the freezer. On the landing, the hot press and the cupboard under the stairs.

Do not tidy before you photograph. A room as it is tells us what the day will involve; a room tidied for the camera does not. If a room is staying as it is for a buyer or a tenant, photograph it anyway and say so.

The Places People Forget

When a quote and the job on the day differ, it is nearly always because one of these was left out.

  • The attic. Photograph up through the hatch with the phone light, and tell us how you get up: a fixed ladder, a stepladder, or nothing. If you cannot get up safely, say so, and we treat it as unseen rather than assuming it is empty.
  • The shed and the garage. From the doorway and in sections, with anything heavy shown on its own.
  • The garden. Garden furniture, pots, a trampoline, piles of timber, a coal bunker or an oil tank. We take the first group; the tank is not ours to take.
  • The side passage and the back of the house, where things go "for now".
  • A car, a trailer or another household's boxes stored at the house. Say what belongs to the job.

Items That Need Their Own Photograph

A few things change how a day is planned and should each get a picture and a line of description: a piano, which we do collect; a safe; a chest freezer or an American-style fridge; a wardrobe or bed that will not come apart; exercise equipment; a dresser; anything upstairs that clearly did not come up the stairs in one piece. Photograph the item and then its route out, including any turn on the stairs.

Photograph the things we cannot take as well, so we can say so in advance: paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries and gas cylinders. They are separated before the day and go to a civic amenity site or back to the supplier; see household versus hazardous waste. Anything that may contain asbestos should not be moved until a qualified person has looked at it.

The Street: Where Will the Truck Stand?

The last photograph is the one most people leave out. Cross the road and photograph the front of the house with the street in shot, so we can see where a box truck can stand, how far it is to the door, and whether there are steps, a gate or a narrow path. Then tell us what the photograph cannot show: whether parking is restricted and when, whether neighbours' cars fill the street at certain times, and whether a rear lane gives better access.

Both towns have an older centre and newer estates, and they are different jobs. Wicklow Town's older streets around the harbour and the main streets, and the older part of Arklow's centre, tend to mean tighter frontages, less room for a truck and a longer carry. The estates built around both towns as they grew usually have a driveway, a garage and an attic: easy parking, and a surprising amount stored out of sight. Tell us which you are dealing with and we plan the truck position in advance.

What to Tell Us in Words

Photographs show the house; a short message fills in the rest:

  • the area, and whether it is an estate house, a town-centre house or an apartment;
  • what is staying, for whom, and how it is labelled;
  • what has been removed since the photographs, or will be before the day;
  • the date the house has to be empty, and whether only a Saturday suits you;
  • who holds the keys, whether there is an alarm, and whether the electricity is on;
  • anything damp, hazardous, or that you are not sure about.

What Changes a Quote on the Day, and How We Avoid It

The written price is the price for the job you showed us, and nothing is added on the day. The only way a quote and a job come apart is when they are different jobs: a locked room nobody mentioned that turns out to be full, an attic that was never photographed, a shed packed to the roof behind a door that did not open. When that happens, the space that was not shown is quoted separately rather than argued about on the doorstep, and you decide whether it goes in the same visit or another.

So the aim is completeness, not quality. A dim picture of a full attic is worth more than a good picture of a tidy sitting room. If in doubt, photograph it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many photographs do you need for a house clearance quote in Wicklow Town or Arklow?

Enough to see every room from the doorway with the storage open, the attic, the outbuildings, the garden and the street. A few photographs per room plus the extras, or a slow video walk-through of a few minutes, does the job.

Is a video better than photographs?

Either works. A slow video from the doorway of each room, pausing on open wardrobes and anything heavy, is often quicker and harder to leave something out of. Add photographs of the street and any awkward item.

Do you need to visit the house before quoting?

Usually not. Most houses are quoted accurately from photographs and video. If something is hard to judge from pictures, such as an unreachable attic or a question about access, we say so and arrange to look rather than guess.

What if I cannot get into the attic to photograph it?

Tell us. We treat an attic nobody has looked into as unseen rather than assuming it is empty, ask what you know about it, and say in the quote how it has been handled. A photograph up through the hatch with the phone light on is enough if you can manage it safely.

Can the price change on the day?

Nothing is added to the written price for the job you showed us. If a space was not in the photographs at all, such as a locked room or an unmentioned shed, it is quoted separately and you decide.

Do you clear houses in Wicklow Town and Arklow on Saturdays?

Yes. We work Monday to Saturday, 8am to 5pm, and not on Sundays. Say early if a particular Saturday is the only day that suits.

Where will the truck park on an older town-centre street?

Wherever the street allows and as close to the door as possible. Send a photograph of the frontage from across the road, tell us about any restrictions and their hours, and we plan the truck position in advance.
