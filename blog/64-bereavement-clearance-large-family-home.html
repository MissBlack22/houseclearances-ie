Bereavement Clearance in Dublin, When the House Is Big

A small house after a death is hard enough. A large one is the same grief with more rooms. Four or five bedrooms, a sitting room and a dining room, a kitchen extension, an attic, a garage and a shed: each one has to be gone through, and in a house where a family grew up, most of them hold something somebody wants.

Our page on house clearance in Dublin explains our service in general, and our bereavement clearance page covers how we work with families and executors. This page is about the large family home: how to plan it, how the family can choose without falling out, and how the clearance runs over more than one day.

Before Anything Is Cleared

  • Talk to the solicitor. Whether anything has to be kept, listed or valued for the estate, and when the house can be cleared. Our guide on clearing a house before probate explains the usual questions. Nothing here is legal advice.
  • Find the papers. Will, deeds, bank books, pension and insurance letters. They are usually in one bureau or drawer, but not always.
  • Agree who decides. One person in the family to answer the crew's questions on the day.
  • Agree how the family chooses. Before the day, not on it. More on that below.

A Large Family Home, Room by Room

Each part of the house asks something different of the family.

The Children's Old Bedrooms

School books, trophies, posters and the things that were never collected when everyone moved out. Before the day, ask each of your brothers and sisters to go through their own old room, or to tell you what they want from it. Whatever is left can then be cleared without anyone finding later that their things went.

The Sitting Room and the Dining Room

The good furniture, the display cabinet, the china, the pictures on the walls. This is where most family disagreements happen, so it helps to choose here first and in turns. A dining table that nobody has room for is often exactly what someone setting up a home needs, and we can see it donated.

The Attic, the Garage and the Shed

Where forty years of a family ended up: cots, prams, Christmas decorations, suitcases, tools and the boxes from the last house. These are usually cleared first, on the first day, because they need the least choosing and they free up the landing, the hall and the drive for the rest of the house.

Choosing Without Falling Out

In a large house with several children, the choosing can be harder than the clearing. A few simple ways families make it fair:

  • Take turns. Each person picks one thing, then the next person, around the family until nobody wants anything else.
  • Use coloured stickers. One colour for each person, on anything they want. Anything without a sticker is cleared.
  • Photograph the house first. For a brother or sister abroad, photos of every room let them choose from wherever they are.
  • One holding room. Everything chosen goes into one room, with the door closed, until it is collected. Nothing in it is loaded.

How the Clearance Runs Over More Than One Day

A full large house is often more than a day's work. We plan it with you before the first day, so you know what will happen when.

  • First day. The attic, the garage, the shed and the rooms already chosen from.
  • Following day or days. The bedrooms, then the main rooms, then the kitchen last, so there is somewhere to make a cup of tea until the end.
  • The holding room. Left alone until the family has collected what they chose.
  • At the end. Every room swept, the house locked, and photos sent if you are not there.

If the family needs a pause in the middle, for a valuer, for a brother flying home or simply for time, the clearance can stop and start again.

Part of Our Job Is to Donate

A large family home has more good furniture and household things than any family can take. Part of our job is to donate. When we clear a home, furniture and household things that are clean and still have use in them are set aside and passed on, to individuals who need them and to charity organisations. If the family would like something in particular donated rather than cleared, tell us and we set it aside separately.

We do not name the charities we work with, and nothing the family has chosen is ever given away. Whether a particular piece can be donated depends on its condition and on whether someone can use it, so we do not promise it for any one item. What cannot be donated is recycled where possible, and the rest goes to an authorised waste facility under our permit, NWCPO-25-13287-01.

What We Take, and What We Cannot

We take

  • Furniture, beds and mattresses
  • Wardrobes, chests of drawers and display cabinets
  • Clothes, bedding, books and kitchenware
  • Appliances and electricals
  • The attic, the garage and the shed
  • Garden furniture and general household waste

We cannot take

  • Paint, varnish and wood stain
  • Solvents, oils and fuel
  • Chemicals
  • Batteries
  • Gas cylinders, full or empty
  • Asbestos or clinical waste

We stand the things we cannot take together and tell you where they are. Equipment supplied on loan, such as a hospital bed or a mobility aid, should go back to whoever supplied it, so check before the day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to clear a large family home?

It depends on how full the house is and how much the family is keeping. A full large house is often more than a day's work. We plan the days with you before we start, and the quote covers all of them.

Can the family take things during the clearance?

Yes. Everything chosen goes into one holding room, and nothing in it is loaded. It can be collected during the clearance or after it.

Who do you take instructions from?

From one named person in the family, or the executor. The others can be asked, but on the day the crew needs one answer to each question.

What happens to papers and photographs?

Documents, photographs and anything personal that turns up are set aside and handed back, never loaded on a guess.

What happens to good furniture nobody in the family wants?

Furniture and household things that are clean and still have use in them are set aside to be donated, to individuals who need them and to charity organisations, where that can be arranged. If there is something the family would like donated, tell us and we set it aside separately.

Can the clearance pause in the middle?

Yes. If the family needs time for a valuer, for someone travelling home, or simply time, the clearance can stop and start again.

Do we need to be there?

Not for the whole job. Many families live elsewhere. We can work from a key, and we send photos when each part is done.

Can the house be cleared before probate?

That is a question for the solicitor. Our guide on clearing a house before probate explains the usual position, but it is not legal advice.
