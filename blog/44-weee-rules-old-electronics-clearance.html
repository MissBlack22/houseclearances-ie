WEEE Rules: What Actually Happens to Old Electronics During a Clearance

Almost every house clearance turns up a drawer or a box of electronics nobody has looked at in years: old chargers, a broken toaster, a television that was replaced but never removed. None of it belongs in the general waste bin. In Ireland, electrical and electronic items are covered by WEEE rules (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment), and they are treated differently from ordinary household rubbish. Here is what that actually means, and what happens to electronics during a clearance.

What Counts as WEEE

WEEE covers anything that runs on a plug or a battery. In a typical house that includes televisions, fridges, freezers, washing machines, microwaves, kettles and toasters, computers, phones, hair dryers, power tools, children's electronic toys, smoke alarms, and the chargers and leads that go with all of it. Some of these items contain materials that should not go to landfill, including refrigerant gases in fridges and freezers, and metals that can be recovered and reused. That is the reason behind the separate rules, not an arbitrary piece of red tape.

Your Right to Return Old Electronics to a Retailer

Under the WEEE Regulations, retailers in Ireland have to take back an old electrical item free of charge when you buy a similar new one, on a like-for-like basis. That applies whether you collect the new item in store or have it delivered, and it does not matter whether the old item was originally bought from that retailer. Larger stores (over 400 square metres of electrical retail space) also have to accept small electrical items with no purchase necessary. Keep your receipt if a store asks for it. If you are replacing an appliance anyway, retailer take-back can deal with the old one at no cost before a clearance crew ever needs to touch it.

Civic Amenity Sites

Local authority civic amenity sites are required to accept household WEEE and waste batteries free of charge, with no gate fee for electrical items specifically, even though the same site may charge for other waste types. This is a sensible option for a single large appliance that is not being replaced, or an accumulation of smaller electronics built up over the years. Rules and opening hours vary by local authority, so check your council's own site before a visit.

What Happens During a Clearance

When we clear a house, electrical items are kept separate from general waste rather than going into the same load as broken furniture or garden debris. Working appliances and electronics in reasonable condition are set aside for reuse where realistic, in the same way we look for donation and resale opportunities across everything we clear. Items that cannot be reused go through the proper WEEE recycling channels rather than landfill, under the same compliance obligations covered in our explainer on what a waste collection permit means.

Data on Old Devices

Computers, phones, tablets and some smart appliances can hold personal data. We do not offer data wiping or destruction as part of a clearance, in the same way we do not shred paperwork. If a device held photos, accounts, passwords or other personal information, factory reset it or remove the storage yourself before it leaves the house. This is worth doing even for devices that seem too old to matter: an old laptop or phone can still hold more personal information than people expect.

What We Cannot Take

A handful of items that look electrical are handled differently. Gas bottles, fuel and chemicals are not WEEE and cannot go through a clearance at all; see our guide to household versus hazardous waste for where those belong. An older-style smoke alarm with a built-in radioactive source needs a specific disposal route, so flag any unusual alarm to the crew rather than assuming it is routine WEEE.

A Few Practical Points

  • Do not bin electricals with general waste. Even a small item like a hair dryer or a charger should go through a WEEE route.
  • Replacing an appliance is a good moment to use retailer take-back first, if the old item is the only thing going.
  • A full garage or attic often holds more WEEE than expected: old chargers, speakers, routers and toys add up. Tell us if there is a lot of it, so we can plan for it.
  • Working items are worth separating out and mentioning in your quote photos, since a device that still works is more useful reused than recycled.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you take an old fridge or washing machine during a house clearance?

Yes, once it is disconnected. Fridges and freezers contain refrigerant and are handled through WEEE recycling rather than general waste.

Do I need to wipe my old computer or phone before you collect it?

Yes. We do not offer data wiping, so please factory reset devices or remove storage yourself beforehand.

Is there a charge for recycling electronics at a civic amenity site?

No. Local authority sites must accept household WEEE and batteries free of charge, though other waste types at the same site may be charged for.
