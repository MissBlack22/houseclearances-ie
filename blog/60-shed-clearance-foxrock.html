Shed Clearance in Foxrock

Foxrock is a suburb of detached houses on generous plots, and a generous plot means a real garden: lawns, beds, fruit trees, and a shed big enough to work in. For forty years one person in the house knew where everything in that shed was. Now the house is being sold, or a parent has moved somewhere smaller, or they are still at home and can no longer manage the garden, and the shed has to be dealt with by people who have never needed to look inside it.

Our shed clearance page describes the service in general, and our page on house clearance in Foxrock covers the area. This page is about one kind of shed: a gardener's shed that belonged to a parent, and how to empty it without losing the things that matter.

What We Do, and What We Do Not

It is better to be plain about this at the start. We clear the contents of a shed. We do not dismantle or remove the shed itself.

  • What you get. Everything you do not want is carried out, the shelves are emptied and the floor is swept. The shed is left standing, empty and ready for whoever has the garden next.
  • What is outside our work. Taking a shed down and carrying it away. If the shed has to go, that needs someone else, and it is easier for them once we have emptied it.
  • A shed in poor repair. Rusted metal, rotten timber and things that have sat in the damp for years are normal for this kind of job. If the roof or the floor has given way, show us in your photos, so the crew knows what it is walking into.

What Is in a Gardener's Shed

It sorts into three groups, and only the last of them is ours.

The Tools: Offer Them Before Anything Else

A spade with a worn handle, a pair of secateurs, a trowel, the good fork. These were chosen with care and looked after, and they are the things a son, a daughter or a grandchild is glad to have. Ask each member of the family to pick one or two they will really use.

What is left of the good tools can be offered to a neighbour who gardens, a relative with a new garden or a community garden. Ask first whether they want them. Whatever nobody takes, we set aside for reuse where possible.

The Garden Things: Decide by the Garden

Pots, canes, netting, seed trays, watering cans, a wheelbarrow, the garden furniture and its cushions. Whether these stay depends on what happens to the garden. If the house is being sold, a few sound pots and a tidy shed help it show well, and the rest can go. If a parent is staying, keep what they still use and put it where they can reach it.

Pots with plants growing in them are part of the garden. We leave them where they are unless you tell us otherwise.

Everything Else: We Take It Away

The mower that has not started in years, hoses that have perished, broken chairs, timber kept for a job that was never done, boxes from the house that were put in the shed for now. Once the family has had its look, this goes out through the side of the house and onto the truck.

Usable items go to reuse and donation where possible, metal is recycled, and the rest is disposed of properly. Personal papers and photographs that turn up in a box are set aside and handed back.

What Has to Stay With You

A gardener's shed always has a shelf of tins and bottles, and that shelf is the one part of the job we cannot do.

We take

  • Hand tools, power tools and workbenches
  • A lawnmower or strimmer with the fuel emptied
  • Pots, canes, hoses and garden furniture
  • Bicycles, toys and sports gear
  • Timber, shelving and old fence panels
  • Boxes, furniture and household items stored in the shed

We cannot take

  • Paint, varnish and wood stain
  • Solvents, oils and fuel
  • Weedkiller, slug pellets and other chemicals
  • Batteries
  • Gas cylinders, full or empty
  • Asbestos or clinical waste

We stand the things we cannot take together in one place and tell you where they are, so you can bring them to a civic amenity site or a hazardous waste collection, or return a cylinder to a supplier. If the shed roof is old corrugated cement sheeting, it is left untouched, because it may contain asbestos and needs a specialist contractor.

How the Day Is Run

  • Someone from the family at the start, if possible. Ten minutes at the shed door, pointing out what is staying, saves a great many questions.
  • Everything comes out into the light. A shed cannot be sorted from the inside. Things are carried out so they can be seen.
  • Kept things go where you say. To the garage, to the house or into the boot of a car, before the rest is loaded.
  • A parent who is at home decides. If your mother or father still lives in the house, nothing leaves without their yes. It is their shed.
  • The shed is swept and the door is closed. If nobody can be there at the end, we send photos of it empty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you take the shed down as well?

No. We clear the contents of the shed, not the structure. If the shed itself has to be taken down or removed, that is outside what we offer, and it is an easier job for whoever does it once the shed is empty.

The shed is in a bad way. Can you still empty it?

Yes. Rusted metal, rotten timber and things that have sat in a damp shed for years are normal for this kind of job. If the roof or the floor has given way, show us in your photos so the crew can plan for it.

Can the family go through it before you start?

Yes, and it is the best way to do it. Either take what you want before the day, or be there at the start and choose as things are carried out. Anything we are unsure about is shown to you before it is loaded.

What happens to good tools that nobody in the family wants?

Working tools and garden furniture in decent condition are set aside for reuse and donation where possible. Metal that cannot be reused is recycled.

Do you take paint, weedkiller and old petrol?

No. We cannot take paint, solvents, oils, fuel or chemicals, and that includes weedkiller and white spirit. We stand them together and tell you where they are, so you can bring them to a civic amenity site or a hazardous waste collection.

My father still lives in the house. Does he get a say?

It is his shed, so yes. Nothing leaves without his agreement, and what he still uses is put wherever he can reach it most easily.

There is no side gate. How do you get things out?

Tell us when you send photos. If the only way from the garden to the road is through the house, we plan the job that way and bring what is needed to protect the floors.

Can the garage or the garden be done on the same visit?

Yes. Send photos of each and we give you one quote for the lot. A shed, a garage and a garden are often cleared together when a house is being prepared for sale.

Do we need to be there?

Not for the whole job. We can work from a key or a gate code, or a neighbour can let us in, and we send photos when the shed is empty. A few minutes with someone from the family at the start is still worth having if it can be managed.
