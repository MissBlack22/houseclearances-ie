House Clearance and Donations in Dublin

A house clearance is often pictured as everything going into the back of a truck and off to a dump. It does not have to be like that, and with us it is not. Part of our job is to donate. The furniture and household things that are clean and still have use in them go on to people who need them, and to charity organisations that pass them on.

Our page on house clearance in Dublin explains our service in general, and our guide to what happens to your stuff after a clearance follows each kind of item to where it ends up. This page is about the donations: what we pass on, who to, and how you can help more of it go to a good home.

Who the Donations Go To

  • Individuals who need them. Someone setting up a first home, a family starting again, a person moving into an empty flat with nothing in it.
  • Charity organisations. Charity shops, furniture projects and groups that help people furnish a home.

We do not name the charities and organisations we work with. What matters is that good furniture is used again, not whose name is over the door.

What a Family Home Can Give

Each charity decides what it can accept, and what one cannot take another sometimes can. These are the things that most often go on to be used again.

Furniture in Good Order

Solid tables and chairs, chests of drawers, bedside lockers, bookcases and wardrobes. Sofas and armchairs that are clean, undamaged and not worn through. A dining table nobody in the family has room for is often exactly what a new home needs.

The Everyday Things

Plates, cups, glasses, cutlery, pots and pans. Clean bedding, towels and curtains, and especially anything still in its packet, which is some of the most useful of all. Lamps, mirrors and pictures.

Books, Games and Toys

Books in good condition, board games and jigsaws with all their pieces, toys that are clean and complete. Children's things from a family home often have years of use left in them.

What Usually Cannot Be Donated

Some things do not have another life in them, however well they were looked after, and it is better to be honest about that.

  • Furniture that is broken, wobbly or missing parts.
  • Anything damp, mouldy, stained or smelling of smoke.
  • Mattresses, pillows and duvets that have been slept on for years.
  • Upholstery that is torn or worn through.
  • Old electricals that may not be safe.

These are not simply dumped. Metal, cardboard and electricals are separated for recycling, and what is left goes to an authorised waste facility under our permit, NWCPO-25-13287-01. Whether a particular piece can be donated also depends on whether someone can use it at the time, so we never promise it for any one item. We set it aside and do our best.

How the Sorting Happens on the Day

  • The family's things are set aside first. Everything being kept goes into one room, or is clearly marked, and nothing in it is loaded.
  • Papers and photographs are kept apart. Anything personal that turns up is set aside and handed back.
  • Donations are loaded separately. So good furniture travels safely and is not damaged on the way.
  • The rest is sorted too. For recycling, and only then for disposal.

If there is something specific you would like donated rather than cleared, furniture in good condition for example, tell us and we set it aside separately.

How the Family Can Help More of It Go to a Good Home

  • Leave furniture where it is. Pieces dragged outside or left in the rain are often past donating by the day of the clearance.
  • Keep sets together. A table with its chairs, a bed with its slats and bolts, a game with all its pieces.
  • Do not throw out the unused things. New bedding, towels and kitchenware are some of the most useful donations of all.
  • Give some things yourselves. If there is a charity or a cause the family knows, give what you want to give before the day. We clear what is left.

What We Cannot Take at All

We cannot take paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries or gas cylinders, full or empty, and we do not handle asbestos or clinical waste. We stand them together and tell you where they are, so they can go to a civic amenity site or a hazardous waste collection, and a gas cylinder back to a supplier.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you donate things from house clearances?

Yes. Part of our job is to donate. Furniture and household things that are clean and still have use in them are set aside and passed on, to individuals who need them and to charity organisations, wherever we can arrange it.

Which charities do you give to?

We do not name the charities and organisations we pass things to. What matters to most families is that good furniture is used again, and that is what we arrange.

Can I ask for particular things to be donated?

Yes. If there is something specific you would like donated rather than cleared, tell us when you send photos and we set it aside separately.

Can everything in good condition be donated?

Not always. Each charity decides what it can accept, and whether a piece can be passed on depends on its condition and on whether someone can use it at the time. So we never promise it for any one item.

What happens to things that cannot be donated?

Metal, cardboard and electricals are separated for recycling, and what is left goes to an authorised waste facility under our permit, NWCPO-25-13287-01.

Will anything we are keeping be given away by mistake?

No. Everything the family is keeping goes into one room or is clearly marked, and nothing in it is loaded. Papers and photographs that turn up are set aside and handed back.

I am a charity. How do I get in touch?

Call us on 083 090 4545. Tell us what you can use and what you cannot, and when a clearance turns up suitable furniture or household items, we can be in touch.
