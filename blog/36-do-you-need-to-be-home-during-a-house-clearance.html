Do You Need to Be Home During a House Clearance?

It's one of the first practical questions people ask once they've booked a clearance: do I have to take the day off and stand there while it happens? The short answer is no, not usually. Most clearances across Dublin go ahead with the homeowner, tenant or family member somewhere else entirely, as long as access and instructions are sorted out beforehand. What matters is getting a few things right in advance, not standing in the hallway all day.

The Short Answer: Usually, No

A clearance team doesn't need supervision to do the job properly. Once a quote is agreed and a date is set, the main things a crew needs are a way into the property and a clear idea of what's going and what's staying. Plenty of our jobs happen while the person who booked them is at work, travelling, or living in a different county altogether. What changes things isn't whether you're present, it's whether the instructions are clear before the van arrives.

What to Sort Out Before the Day

If you won't be there, a short conversation beforehand covers almost everything:

  • Access: how keys will reach the team, and how they'll get back to you (or to a solicitor, estate agent or neighbour) afterwards.
  • Scope: every room, or only some of them. Say so clearly, including the attic, garage or shed if there is one — it's easy to forget these when you're not walking the house with the crew.
  • What stays: anything you're keeping should be moved to one room, or clearly labelled, so there's no risk of it going with everything else.
  • A contact number: for the one or two questions that come up on the day, like whether a particular item is wanted or not.

How Access Usually Works Without You There

There's no single answer here, it depends on the situation. Some people leave a key with a neighbour. Some arrange collection from an estate agent or a solicitor's office, which is common when a clearance is tied to a sale or a bereavement. Others use a keysafe. Whatever the arrangement, agree it with the clearance company before the day, not on the morning of, and confirm how the keys get back to you once the job is finished.

Tell the team about anything that isn't obvious from the outside: an alarm that needs disarming, a tricky lock, a gate code, or a parking restriction on the street. None of that needs you to be present, it just needs to be written down somewhere the crew can see it.

What to Set Aside First

Whether or not you're there, it's worth going through a property yourself first for anything personal, financial or potentially valuable: documents, jewellery, photographs, and paperwork like bank statements or a will. A clearance team will usually set aside anything that obviously looks important if they come across it, but you know your own house better than anyone walking through it for the first time. Taking an hour to check drawers, bedside tables and coat pockets before the day removes any doubt.

When It's Worth Being There

Being present isn't necessary, but it can make sense in a few situations:

  • You haven't finished deciding what you want to keep, and would rather make those calls room by room as the team works.
  • The house hasn't been sorted at all, and you want to point out specific items as they come up.
  • It's the first time back in the property since a bereavement, and being there on the day feels right for you, even if it isn't strictly needed for the job.
  • There's something unusual about the property, like an outbuilding or a part of the house that's easy to miss, that's simpler to show in person than to describe.

None of these are requirements, they're just situations where it tends to help. If you'd rather be there for your own peace of mind, that's a perfectly good reason on its own.

Apartments and Managed Buildings

Flats and apartments add a layer that houses don't have: lifts that need booking, loading-bay rules, and a management company that may restrict when work can happen in communal areas. You still don't need to be present yourself, but someone needs to confirm the building's rules in advance, whether that's you, a letting agent, or whoever manages the block. Working at a Distance

This comes up often with families managing a clearance from another county, or another country. It's entirely possible to arrange a quote from photos or a short video, agree a date by phone or WhatsApp, pass on access instructions, and have the property confirmed clear afterwards without ever setting foot in it yourself.
