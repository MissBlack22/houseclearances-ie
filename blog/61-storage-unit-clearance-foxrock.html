Storage Unit Clearance in Foxrock

Foxrock houses are large, and a large house holds a great deal of furniture: a long dining table, large wardrobes, a sideboard, two or three sofas, beds for children who left long ago. When the house is sold and a parent moves to an apartment or a smaller house, only part of it goes with them. Nobody wants to decide about the rest on the week of a move, so it goes into storage.

Our storage unit clearance page describes the service in general, and our page on house clearance in Foxrock covers the area. This page is about that particular unit, the one that holds what is left of a family home, and how a family brings itself to close it.

Why the Unit Is Still There

It is rarely laziness. Three things keep a unit like this open.

  • The furniture is good. It was bought to last and it has lasted. Paying to keep it feels more respectful than letting it go.
  • It came from home. The table is the one every Christmas was eaten at. Deciding about it feels like deciding about the house all over again.
  • Nobody has been given the job. Each of the family assumes that another will want the sideboard, and nobody has asked.

None of those is a reason to keep paying. They are reasons to ask three questions, in order, and then to act on the answers.

Three Questions That Empty a Unit

Ask them in this order. Each one settles part of the unit, and what is left after the third is ours to clear.

Does the Person It Belonged to Want Anything Back?

Your parent has now lived in the new place for a while and knows what is missed. It may be a single chair, a lamp or the bedside locker that was the right height. Bring photos of the unit to them, not them to the unit, and let them choose.

Measure the space in the new home before anything is promised. What they choose is set to one side in the unit, and the family brings it over.

Does Anyone in the Family Have Room for It?

Send the same photos to everyone at once, with one date a few weeks ahead. Ask each person for one of two answers about each piece: I will collect it by that date, or let it go. A third answer, keep it for me, is how the unit came to be full in the first place.

Anything claimed is marked with a name on a strip of tape. Whatever has a name on it when we arrive is left exactly where it is.

Is It Good Enough for Someone Else to Use?

This is the question families worry about most, because nobody wants to see a parent's furniture thrown out. Pieces in good condition are set aside for reuse and donation where possible. That is part of every clearance we do, and it is not left to chance.

One honest point: not everything that is good is wanted. Very large wardrobes and heavy suites can be hard to place. We will tell you plainly which pieces are likely to be reused, so the family is not left wondering.

A Smaller Unit, or No Unit at All

Closing the account is not the only choice. Some families keep a few pieces for a grandchild's first home, or for a brother who is abroad for another year.

Close the account

Move to a smaller unit

  • Tell us what is being kept and what is going

If you choose the smaller unit, put a date on that too. A box kept for a grandchild is a kindness. A second unit that nobody visits is the first unit again.

At the Storage Facility

Storage facilities have their own access rules, and no two run things in quite the same way. Tell us which facility the unit is in and what access involves, and we fit our time to their hours.

  • Whose name is on the account. If it is your parent's, make sure you have the access and authorisation the facility needs before the day.
  • Access hours. Some facilities are open long hours, and others keep to a set window and expect notice when a crew and a truck are coming.
  • The loading bay. If it has to be booked, book it as soon as the date is agreed.
  • Lifts and trolleys. A unit on an upper floor means a lift or a trolley run, so tell us the floor.
  • Codes, cards and ID. If reception needs any of these, tell us what is involved when you book.

What We Take and What We Cannot

We take household furniture, beds and mattresses, boxes of books and kitchenware, ornaments, pictures, rugs, lamps, appliances and whatever else came out of the house. We cannot take paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries, gas cylinders, asbestos or clinical waste. If any of those turn up in the unit, we stand them to one side and tell you.

Old paperwork and photograph albums are often boxed up with the rest. Personal papers and photographs are set aside as we find them and handed to the family.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need to see the unit before you quote?

Usually not. The unit size, as listed by the storage provider, with a photo of the open unit or a rough description of what is in it, is normally enough. A very large or complicated unit sometimes needs a quick look first.

The account is in my mother's name. Can I arrange the clearance?

Yes. Make sure you have the access and authorisation the facility needs, and we take it from there. Ask the facility what they require, because each one handles it in its own way.

Can you clear part of the unit and leave the rest?

Yes. If the family is moving to a smaller unit, or keeping a few pieces, tell us what is staying and what is going, and we clear only the contents that are leaving.

What happens to the good furniture?

Pieces in good condition are set aside for reuse and donation where possible. What cannot be reused is recycled where it can be, and the rest goes to an authorised waste facility.

My sister wants the dining table and cannot collect it for a month. What do we do?

Put her name on it. Anything marked is left in the unit. If the account is being closed, the piece has to be collected before the last day, so give her that date in writing.

Do we need to be there?

Not necessarily, as long as the facility's access arrangements allow it and we have agreed in advance what is being kept and what is being cleared. Many customers come for a walk-through at the start and then leave us to it.

Nobody has opened the unit in over a year. Does that change anything?

No. We work from the unit size and whatever you can tell us, clear it in the same way and sort the contents for reuse, recycling or disposal as we go.

Can the house, the garage or the attic be done as well?

Yes. Tell us what else needs clearing and we quote for the whole job together.
