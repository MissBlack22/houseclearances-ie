Property Clearance: A Guide for Landlords, Executors and Property Managers

"Property clearance" and "house clearance" get used almost interchangeably, but the people searching for them are usually in quite different positions. Someone searching "house clearance" is often clearing their own home, or a family member's, and dealing with the contents themselves. Someone searching "property clearance" is more often acting on someone else's behalf — a landlord clearing a unit a tenant has vacated, a letting agent managing that on a landlord's behalf, a solicitor or executor dealing with a deceased person's estate, or a receiver or property manager preparing a vacant property for sale or re-letting. The property being cleared usually isn't, and often never was, the home of the person arranging the clearance. That distinction shapes almost everything about how the job needs to run — from how access is arranged to who actually needs to sign off on the work. This guide is written for that audience: what property clearance covers as a service, what to look for in a property clearance company, and how the work tends to differ depending on whether you're a landlord, a solicitor, an executor, a receiver or a property manager.

What "Property Clearance" Actually Means

At its core, property clearance is the removal of everything left in a property — furniture, belongings, rubbish, fixtures that aren't staying — so the property can be handed over, sold, re-let or simply made safe and presentable again. What sets it apart from a standard house clearance isn't really the physical work, which is largely the same: furniture out, rubbish sorted, the space left clean and empty. It's the relationship. In a typical house clearance, the person who owns the belongings is usually the person instructing the clearance, or a close family member acting for them. In a property clearance, the person instructing the work is very often not the person who owned or used the property at all — a landlord instructing a clearance on a property they don't live in, a solicitor instructing one on behalf of an estate, a receiver instructing one on a property they've only recently taken control of. That third-party relationship means more of the job happens over email and phone calls, more of it depends on someone else supplying keys or access, and more of it needs to be explainable afterwards — to a landlord client, to beneficiaries of an estate, to a lender.

It also tends to be less sentimental and more operational. A homeowner clearing their own house is often making individual decisions about individual items. A landlord, agent, solicitor or receiver instructing a clearance is usually making one decision — clear the property — and expects the company doing the work to handle the judgment calls within that instruction, reporting back only where something genuinely needs a decision (a locked safe, a box of documents, something that looks like it might have value). Property clearance, in other words, is house clearance work delivered as a managed service rather than a personal one.

What a Property Clearance Company Actually Does

The scope of the work varies more than it does for a straightforward domestic house clearance, because the properties themselves vary more — a one-bed rental flat, a five-bedroom family home going through probate, a warehouse unit, a shop, a whole terrace of units under one receivership. In practice, a property clearance company is generally handling some combination of the following:

  • Full clearances — everything in the property removed in one visit, down to an empty, swept space.
  • Partial clearances — a single room, a garage, a shed or an attic, where the rest of the property isn't part of the instruction.
  • Furniture and bulky item removal — sofas, beds, wardrobes, appliances, often the majority of the physical volume in a job.
  • General rubbish and accumulated waste — everything from bin bags to years of accumulated clutter, depending on how the property was left.
  • Responsible sorting and disposal — separating what can be donated or recycled from what genuinely needs to go to waste, and disposing of it through a properly licensed route.
  • Working via a third party for access — collecting keys from an agent, a solicitor's office, a neighbour, or a lockbox, rather than being let in by the person who used the property, and often reporting back photos or an update once the job is done since the instructing party frequently isn't on site.

That last point is worth dwelling on, because it's one of the most practical differences between property clearance and a typical domestic job. On most house clearances, someone connected to the property is present at some point — to point out what's staying, ask questions, or simply be there. On a property clearance instructed by an agent, solicitor or receiver, it's common for no one who knows the property to be present at all. The company doing the work needs to be comfortable operating from a written brief, competent at flagging anything genuinely ambiguous rather than guessing, and reliable enough that the instructing party doesn't feel they need to attend in person to make sure it's done properly.

How to Choose a Property Clearance Company

Because the person instructing a property clearance is so often acting for someone else — a landlord, a set of beneficiaries, a client — the choice of company carries more weight than it might for a one-off domestic job. A few things are worth checking before you instruct anyone.

A Valid Waste Collection Permit, and Why It Actually Matters to You

Anyone collecting and transporting waste for reward in Ireland is required to hold a waste collection permit, issued through the National Waste Collection Permit Office. This isn't just a box-ticking credential. Waste legislation in Ireland places some responsibility on whoever arranges for waste to be collected, which is why using a properly permitted operator matters — if waste ends up being disposed of illegally by an unlicensed operator, the person or business that arranged the collection can find themselves drawn into the fallout, not just the operator who dumped it. For a landlord, letting agent, solicitor or receiver instructing a clearance on someone else's behalf, that's a real and practical reason to check a permit exists and is current, not just a formality. HouseClearances.ie is a fully insured waste carrier holding Waste Collection Permit No. NWCPO-25-13287-01, and we're happy to confirm that to anyone instructing a job before it's booked.

Insurance

Clearance work involves being inside a property that isn't the clearance company's own — moving furniture through hallways and doorways, working around fixtures and fittings, sometimes in an occupied or partly-occupied building. Confirming a company carries appropriate insurance before they're on site, rather than after something's gone wrong, is basic due diligence, and any legitimate operator should be able to confirm it without hesitation.

Ability to Handle Volume, and Multiple Properties

A property clearance company needs to be able to scale to the job — a single flat is a different undertaking to a five-bedroom house or a warehouse unit, and a company that's only ever done small domestic jobs may not have the crew, vehicles or scheduling flexibility for something larger. If you're managing more than one property, it's also worth asking whether a company can handle a series of clearances over time rather than treating every instruction as a fresh, unrelated job.

Clear Quoting

A property clearance company that won't give you a straight answer on how a quote is put together is harder to budget against, and harder to explain to a client or a set of beneficiaries afterwards. Look for a company that's willing to quote from photos or a description before committing to a site visit, and that can explain plainly what affects the price — volume, access, and how much sorting is involved — rather than a vague number with no basis.

References and a Track Record

Genuine reviews and a visible track record of similar work are worth more here than they might for a one-off personal job, because you're often putting your own name or your firm's name behind the choice of contractor. A company that's used regularly by agents, solicitors or property managers, and that can point to that experience, is a reasonable proxy for reliability.

Comfortable Working With a Third Party

Finally, and specifically for this kind of work: does the company have experience taking instruction from someone who isn't the person who used the property — coordinating keys with an agent, working to a solicitor's timeline, reporting back to someone who wasn't on site? Not every clearance operator is set up for that. It's a genuinely different way of working from turning up to a homeowner's front door, and it's worth asking about directly.

Property Clearance for Landlords and Letting Agents

For landlords and letting agents, property clearance is most often about turning a rental property around between tenancies — clearing what a previous tenant has left behind so the property can be cleaned, checked and re-listed with as little vacant time as possible. Every day a unit sits full is a day it isn't earning rent, so speed is usually the priority alongside the clearance itself. We cover the specifics of rental turnarounds — deposit and handover deadlines, HMO clearances, standing arrangements for agents managing several units — in more detail on our end of tenancy clearance page.

Belongings a tenant has left behind are one of the most common reasons a property clearance gets booked in the first place. We can clear whatever's left as part of the job, sorted for donation, recycling or disposal in the usual way — but we don't advise on the legal position around abandoned tenant property itself. What notice is appropriate, how long items should be held, and what a landlord is entitled to do varies by situation, and that's genuinely a question for your own solicitor or the relevant guidance, not something a clearance company should be telling you. Once that's settled on your end, the physical clearance is straightforward.

If you're managing several properties rather than one, the more useful conversation is usually about a standing arrangement — a single point of contact, consistent pricing, and priority scheduling around your turnaround dates, rather than a fresh negotiation every time a unit changes hands. That's covered in more depth on the end of tenancy clearance page too, but it applies just as much to broader property clearance instructions as it does to residential lettings specifically.

Property Clearance for Solicitors and Executors

Estate clearances bring a different set of pressures. The property itself is often not being cleared by the person who lived in it, and the family — where there is one closely involved — may be grieving, geographically scattered, or simply not in a position to manage the physical work themselves. Solicitors and executors instructing a clearance in this situation usually need two things above all: a company that will work sensitively and at a reasonable pace rather than rushing through a family home, and a company that can be trusted to operate correctly when no family member is present to supervise every decision.

Estate clearances are also frequently tied to timelines that are set elsewhere — a Grant of Probate coming through, a property going to market, a deadline set by beneficiaries or the estate's own affairs — rather than a date the clearance company or even the executor gets to choose freely. Whether a property can be cleared before probate is finalised is a legal question that depends on the specifics of the estate, and it's one worth confirming with a solicitor rather than assuming either way; once that's settled, we can work to whatever date follows from it. Our bereavement clearance page covers how we approach this kind of work in more detail, and our executor's checklist guide covers the wider process an executor is usually working through, of which clearing the property is only one part.

In practice, a lot of estate clearance instructions come through a solicitor's office rather than directly from a family member — the solicitor holds the keys, coordinates timing with beneficiaries, and wants a straightforward update once the job is done. That's a normal way for this work to happen, and one we're set up for.

Property Clearance for Receivers and Property Managers

A different, more commercial version of the same need comes from receivers, asset managers and property managers dealing with vacant properties — a repossessed home ahead of sale, a unit changing hands between owners, or a property being prepared for viewings after standing empty. Here the framing is usually less about sentiment and more about presentation and timeline: getting a property to a state where it can be photographed, viewed and sold or re-let without a buyer or tenant walking into a space full of a previous occupant's belongings.

Receivers in particular often take control of a property with limited background on its history or condition, and need a contractor who can assess what's actually there, clear it efficiently, and report back clearly — sometimes across a number of properties taken on around the same time. The same standing-arrangement approach that applies to letting agents managing a portfolio applies here too: a consistent point of contact who already understands how instructions come in and what a finished property needs to look like, rather than starting from scratch with each new unit.

Property Clearance vs House Clearance vs Junk Removal

These three terms overlap enough that it's worth being direct about how they relate, rather than pretending they're entirely separate services. House clearance is the broadest and most consumer-facing of the three — clearing a whole property, most often instructed by the person who lives there or a close family member, frequently tied to downsizing, moving, or a bereavement. Junk removal tends to describe a narrower job — a single item, a specific space like a shed or garage, or a smaller volume of unwanted belongings — regardless of who's instructing it. Property clearance sits across both of these in terms of physical scope, but is defined less by the size of the job and more by who's arranging it and why: a landlord, agent, solicitor, receiver or property manager acting in a professional or administrative capacity, rather than a homeowner dealing with their own belongings.

In practice, we quote and carry out all three the same way — based on what's actually in the property and what needs to happen to it, not on which term was used to describe the enquiry. The distinction matters more for finding the right information up front than it does for how the job itself is priced or delivered.

What's Involved in the Process

The process for a property clearance follows the same basic shape regardless of who's instructing it, with the details adjusted for a third-party relationship rather than a homeowner clearing their own house.

  1. Get in touch with the details. A description of the property, roughly what's in it, and — critically for this kind of instruction — who holds keys or access and how that will be arranged.
  2. Receive a free, no-obligation quote. Photos or a short video are usually enough for an accurate quote without a separate site visit, which matters when the person requesting the quote isn't the one who can walk through the property themselves.
  3. Agree a date, working around access and any deadline. Whether that's a tenancy handover date, a probate-linked timeline, or a sale completion date, we work to the date that matters to the instruction rather than fitting the job in whenever suits us.
  4. Keys and access are arranged. Directly with an agent, a solicitor's office, a lockbox, or whatever arrangement suits the instructing party — the clearance doesn't require the person who used the property to be present.
  5. The property is cleared. Furniture, belongings and rubbish removed, with anything genuinely ambiguous — documents, anything that looks like it might need a decision — flagged rather than assumed.
  6. Items are sorted for donation, recycling or disposal. We aim for an average recycling rate of up to 95%, sending as little as possible to landfill.
  7. The property is left clean and empty, ready for whatever comes next — a new tenant, a sale, a handover — with confirmation sent back to whoever instructed the job.
