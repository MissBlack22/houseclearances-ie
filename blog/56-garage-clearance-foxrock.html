Whose Is It? The Five Owners of a Family Garage

Stand at the door and you can usually sort the contents by owner before you sort them by type.

  • The parents. Garden tools, the lawnmower, spare furniture, paint for rooms that have since been repainted.
  • Each of the children. Bicycles, school and college boxes, sports gear, a guitar amplifier, the contents of a first flat.
  • A parent who has died. A workbench, hand tools, fishing rods, golf clubs. These are the hardest things in the garage to decide about.
  • The grandchildren. A cot, a high chair and a box of toys kept for visits.
  • Nobody. Things minded for a neighbour, a relative abroad or a friend who moved away.

Until each of those people has had a say, a clearance cannot start. That is not a delay. It is the part of the job only the family can do.

A Plan That Is Fair to Everyone

Three steps, done in this order, settle most garages without an argument.

1. One Message, One Date

Photograph the garage from the door and along each wall, and send the same photos to everyone at once. Give one date, a few weeks ahead, and ask each person for one of three answers about their own things: I will collect it, keep it for me, or let it go.

A date matters more than the wording. Without one, a box can sit in a garage for another ten years on the strength of "I'll be over for it".

2. One Corner, With Names On It

Clear a corner, or one length of shelving, and make it the only place in the garage where kept things live. Each item or box gets a strip of masking tape with a name on it. Anything with a name on it stays. Everything else goes.

For a family member who lives abroad, agree a limit that can be posted or stored, such as one box. It is kinder than keeping everything and kinder than keeping nothing.

3. Then the Clearance

Once the date has passed, send us the photos and tell us where the kept corner is. Our crew empties the rest of the garage, takes down shelving if you want it gone, sweeps the floor and sends you photos. The labelled corner is left exactly as it was.

If the attic and the shed are in the same state, they can be done on the same job. See attic clearance and shed clearance.

A Parent's Workshop

The bench, the vice and the wall of hand tools are different from everything else in the garage, because they are the nearest thing to the person. Families often want to keep all of it and have room for none of it.

  • Ask each member of the family to choose one or two tools they will actually use. A hammer that is used is a better keepsake than a tool chest in another garage.
  • Offer the good hand tools to a neighbour, a relative who is starting out, or a community workshop that can use them. Ask first whether they want them.
  • Take a photograph of the bench as it was before anything is moved.
  • Leave the rest to us. Tools and metal in fair condition are set aside for reuse where possible, and what cannot be reused is recycled as scrap.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you leave some things behind for the family to collect later?

Yes. Put them in one corner with a name on each and tell us where that corner is. Nothing labelled is loaded, and we send a photo of the corner when we finish so everyone can see what was kept.

My brother lives abroad and cannot collect his things. What do families usually do?

Most agree a limit, such as one box, and send him photos of everything else so he can choose. Whatever he chooses goes in the labelled corner or into the house. We are a clearance company, so sending items on to another address is something the family arranges.

What happens to good tools and bicycles?

Items in fair condition are set aside for reuse or donation where possible. Metal that cannot be reused goes for scrap recycling. If the family wants particular tools kept, mark them and they stay.

Will you take down the shelving and the workbench?

Yes, if you ask. Freestanding shelving goes as it is, and units fixed to the wall are unscrewed, which leaves the fixing holes behind. Tell us when you send photos so it is included in the price.

Do you take paint, oil or gas cylinders?

No. We cannot take paint, solvents, oils, fuel, chemicals, batteries or gas cylinders. We put them together in one place and tell you where they are.

We found boxes of photographs and letters. Will those be kept?

Yes. Personal papers, photographs and keepsakes that turn up in boxes are set aside and shown to the family. They are not put in the truck.

Can the attic and the shed be cleared on the same visit?

Usually, yes. Send photos of each with the garage and one price covers all of them. We tell you how long to allow when we send the quote.

Do we need to be there?

No. We can work from a key, a code for the garage door or a neighbour who lets us in, and we send photos when the garage is empty.
