What Happens During a House Clearance in Dublin: From First Call to Empty House

Most people only arrange a house clearance once or twice in their lives, and usually at a stressful moment: a bereavement, a sale, a move, the end of a tenancy. That makes it hard to know what is normal. How does the quote work? Do you have to be there? What exactly happens to everything? And what should you have done before the crew arrives?

This guide walks through the process in the order it happens, with the Dublin-specific points that catch people out. It is deliberately about the process. What a House Clearance Is, and What It Is Not

A house clearance means removing the contents of a property, in whole or in part, so that it is empty and ready for whatever comes next. That might be a sale, a new tenant, a renovation, or simply the end of a chapter. It is different from a house move, where your belongings go with you, and different from a skip, where you do the loading yourself. The practical difference is who does the lifting, sorting and disposal. A skip is a container; a clearance company is people, vehicles and a permit to carry waste. Our guide on skip hire versus a clearance service compares the two.

Clearances come in two broad shapes:

  • Full clearance. Everything goes, room by room, down to bare floors. This is the usual request for estates, sales and tenancy handbacks.
  • Partial clearance. One room, the attic, the garage, the shed, or the bulky items only, while the rest stays. Many people start with this when they are not ready to clear everything.

Specific spaces have their own pages: attics, garages, sheds and storage units.

Stage 1: The First Contact and the Quote

It starts with a message or a call. The details that help most at this stage are simple: the area, the type of property (house, apartment, terrace, bungalow), roughly how many rooms, whether there is an attic, garage or shed, and the date you are working to.

The quickest route to an accurate quote is photographs. Photos let a clearance company see:

  • how much furniture and how many belongings there are in each room
  • whether there are large or heavy items such as wardrobes, piano, fridge-freezers or a pool table
  • how easy the property is to reach: stairs, narrow doorways, a long path, steps to the front door
  • the attic, garage, shed and garden, which are often the hardest parts to describe in words

A short video walking through the house works even better. If you cannot be in Dublin, or the property belonged to a relative, photos and a video are usually all that is needed. You can send them on WhatsApp or by the quote form on this page. A free quote is not a commitment.

Some jobs benefit from a viewing, particularly where there is a large amount of belongings, difficult access or something unusual. A good company will tell you honestly whether photos are enough.

Stage 2: Agreeing the Plan

Once the quote is agreed, the details to settle are the date, the access and who decides what. Three questions come up repeatedly:

  • Who holds the keys, and who will be on site? You do not have to be there. Many owners, executors, landlords and agents arrange access and let the crew get on with it.
  • Is there anything that must not be removed? Mark it, move it, or tell the company clearly. Items that stay, such as fitted wardrobes, appliances that are included in a sale, or things the landlord owns, should be agreed in advance.
  • Is there anything you want set aside? Keepsakes, documents, photographs, jewellery, tools. A good crew will set aside anything that looks as though it might matter, but it helps if you say what you are hoping to find.

If the property is part of an estate, check with the solicitor before anything of value is disposed of. See can you clear a house before probate? for the details, and clearing a loved one's home if the job is a family one.

Stage 3: How to Prepare

You do not need to pack boxes or carry anything to the door. The aim of preparing is to make the clearance quicker and make sure nothing important goes by mistake.

  1. Remove what you are keeping. Valuables, documents, keepsakes, important papers, medication, keys and anything sentimental. Do this before the day, not during it.
  2. Check pockets, drawers and the back of cupboards. People are often surprised by what turns up in coat pockets, the top of a wardrobe or the bottom of a bedside drawer.
  3. Empty the fridge and freezer and, if you can, defrost them.
  4. Clear a route. A path to the front door, the attic hatch, the side gate and the shed makes everything faster.
  5. Deal with hazardous items yourself. We cannot take paint, solvents, oils or chemicals, so take them to a civic amenity site or a hazardous waste collection. See household versus hazardous waste.
  6. Sort out parking. See the Dublin section below.

Stage 4: Clearance Day

On the day, the typical sequence is:

  1. Arrival and walk-through. The crew confirms the plan with you or the key-holder: what is going, what is staying, and anything set aside.
  2. Access and protection. The crew sets up a route from the property to the vehicle, and protects floors, hallways or common areas if the building needs it.
  3. Loading, room by room. Furniture and larger items go first so the rooms open up, followed by smaller items and general contents. Furniture is dismantled where it needs to be to get out through doors and stairs.
  4. Sorting as it goes. Items that can be reused, donated or recycled are separated from what has to be disposed of.
  5. Attic, garage, shed and garden. These are often cleared last, once the main house is done.
  6. Final walk-through. The crew checks every room, cupboard and space with you or the key-holder, and the property is left empty and swept of loose debris where agreed.

How long it takes depends on the amount of belongings, the number of people on the job, the access and the distance to disposal, which is why we do not quote durations in the abstract. A clear answer at the quote stage is something to expect from any company.

Stage 5: What Happens to Everything

This is the question most people do not ask until afterwards, and it is worth asking before. In a responsible clearance, items follow a priority order: reuse first where something is in good condition, then recycling for materials such as metal, electricals and cardboard, and disposal only for what is left. Electrical items, mattresses and hazardous materials each have their own routes. For a fuller explanation, see what happens to your belongings after a clearance.

Whoever takes waste away from your property should hold a waste collection permit. If something is dumped illegally, the householder can be held responsible for the waste they handed over. We explain how to check in what a waste collection permit means and in our guide on how to choose a house clearance company.

Stage 6: After the Clearance

When the crew leaves, a few things are worth doing:

  • Walk the property (or ask for photos) to confirm every room, the attic, garage and shed are as agreed.
  • Hand back or collect keys.
  • Take dated photographs of the empty property, particularly for a tenancy or a sale.
  • Think about what comes next: cleaning, repairs, redecoration, or the estate agent. A cleared property is a blank page; it is often the right moment to arrange the next step, such as a rental turnaround.

Dublin-Specific Things to Plan For

Dublin properties vary widely, and local conditions affect a clearance more than most people expect.

  • Terraced houses and period homes. Narrow halls, steep stairs and small landings in older areas such as Rathmines and Portobello mean more carrying and less room to turn large furniture.
  • Parking. Resident permit schemes, pay-and-display and tight streets limit where a vehicle can stop. Tell the company about parking when you book, and speak to neighbours or the street's management if you need space to load.
  • Apartments. Lifts, loading bays and management company rules often decide how the day goes. Our apartment clearance guide covers what to ask the building.
  • Four local authorities. Dublin is served by several councils, and their bulky waste and recycling services differ. Dublin City Council, for example, runs a bulky household waste collection for households in its area. Check your own council's current service, as the terms vary.
  • Properties further out. Distance to the property and to the nearest disposal point affects the time a job takes. Areas like Swords, Tallaght and Dundrum are all part of our regular coverage.

Questions to Ask Any Clearance Company

  • Do you hold a waste collection permit, and can I see the number?
  • Do you carry public liability insurance?
  • Will you give me a written quote, and what does it include?
  • Can you quote from photographs?
  • Will you set aside items I want to keep or check?
  • What happens to the items afterwards?
  • Do I need to be there, and how will keys be handled?
  • What happens if there is more than the quote allowed for?

The last question matters more than it looks. A reliable company will explain, before you book, what happens if the amount differs from the quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to be at the property during a house clearance?

No. Many owners, executors, landlords and agents arrange access and let the crew work. You are welcome to be there if you prefer, to point things out or collect something at the end.

Can I get a quote from photos?

Yes, and it is usually the fastest route. Send clear photos or a short video of every room plus the attic, garage and shed, and note any stairs, access limits and your target date.

What if I find something valuable after the crew has started?

Tell the crew straight away. This is why we suggest removing valuables and documents beforehand and saying what you hope to find, so that it can be set aside.

Can you clear just part of a house?

Yes. A single room, the attic, the garage, the shed or just the bulky items can be cleared on their own.

Can you clear a house before it is sold?

Yes. Many clearances are for sales. Agree with your estate agent or solicitor what stays with the property, and give the crew a clear list.

What happens to the furniture?

Reusable items are separated where possible, with recycling and disposal for the rest. See what happens after a clearance.

How do I check a company is legitimate?

Ask for their waste collection permit number and check it with the issuing authority. Our guide to waste collection permits explains how.
