Attic Clearance in Killiney

Many of the houses on Killiney Hill and the roads around it have been in one family for a very long time. The children left years ago. One parent, or both, is still there, managing well, and has no wish to move. The house suits them, apart from one thing: half of what they use a few times a year is kept in the roof.

Our attic clearance page describes the service in general, and our page on house clearance in Killiney covers the area. This page is about an attic clearance where the house is not being sold and nobody is moving out. The aim is simply an attic that no one needs to go into.

Start With the Person, Not the Attic

It is their attic and their house. A clearance that is arranged around a parent, and not with them, tends to go badly, however kindly it is meant. Three things make the conversation easier.

  • Say what it is for. Not "you have too much stuff" but "I would like you never to have to go up that ladder again".
  • Promise that nothing is decided for them. Every box comes down and is seen before anything is done with it.
  • Agree where the things they still use will live. People let go far more easily once they know the decorations have a new home downstairs.

If a parent is not ready, wait. An attic is not urgent in the way a leaking roof is, and the day goes better when they have chosen it.

Everything in the Attic Falls Into Three Groups

Sorting by where a thing is going, and not by what it is, keeps the decisions simple.

Still Used: It Comes Downstairs to Stay

Christmas decorations, suitcases, spare bedding, the fan for the summer, the box of family photographs that is taken out when the grandchildren visit. These are not cleared. Our crew carries them to wherever has been chosen: the top of a wardrobe is no better than the attic, so think of a spare-room press, the cupboard under the stairs or a shelf at waist height.

Before the day, write a label for each box in large letters. It is a small thing, and it means your parent can find the tree lights without anyone's help.

Belongs to the Family: It Goes Home With Them

School reports, college notes, a wedding dress, a train set, the cot that was kept for grandchildren who are now teenagers. Each of these belongs to a son or daughter, and each of them should decide. Ask everyone to collect their own things, or to say plainly that they can go.

Set a date for that, and put what is waiting to be collected in one room with a name on each box. A parent's attic should not become a parent's spare bedroom.

Everything Else: We Take It Away

Old carpet, broken chairs, boxes for appliances long gone, curtains for windows in another house, a television nobody would give room to. Once your parent has seen it and agreed, it goes down the stairs and out to the truck. Reusable items go to reuse and donation where possible, and the rest is recycled or disposed of properly.

Personal papers and photographs that turn up in boxes are set aside and handed to your parent, whatever group the box was in.

How the Day Is Run

A clearance with the owner at home is paced differently from one in an empty house.

  • A chair, not a ladder. Your parent sits on the landing or in a room downstairs. Everything comes to them.
  • One box at a time. It is opened, looked at and given one of three answers: keep, family, go.
  • Time to stop. There is a break when one is wanted. A photograph album can take ten minutes, and that is allowed.
  • The stairs and landing covered before the first box comes down.
  • A family member there if possible, to carry the kept boxes to their new places and to keep the day calm.
  • No arguments about a maybe. If your parent is unsure, the box is kept. It can always go another day.

When the Attic Is Empty

The attic is swept of loose debris and the hatch is closed. The insulation, the water tanks and the pipework are part of the house and stay exactly as they are. Some families ask us to leave nothing at all up there, so that there is no reason for anyone to open the hatch. Others keep a few light boxes near the hatch for a son or daughter to fetch at Christmas. Either works, as long as your parent is not the one climbing.

Good places for what is kept

  • A press or wardrobe in a spare bedroom, on the lower shelves
  • The cupboard under the stairs
  • A shelf in the utility room
  • A dry shelf in the garage, at waist height

Places to avoid

  • The top of a wardrobe
  • High kitchen presses that need a stool
  • The floor of a room that is used every day
  • Anywhere that means a step, a ladder or a stretch

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my mother have to be there on the day?

It is better if she is, because it is her attic and she can see each thing as it comes down. If she would prefer not to be, a family member can stand in with a list she has agreed.

She is worried that strangers will decide what to throw out. What do we tell her?

That nothing is decided for her. Each box is brought down, opened and shown, and it only goes on the truck when she says so. Anything she is unsure about stays.

Can you carry the kept boxes to wherever she wants them?

Yes. Tell us where each group is going, such as the spare-room press or the cupboard under the stairs, and our crew puts them there before the rest is loaded.

What if she changes her mind about something on the day?

Then it stays. Keeping more than was planned does not add to the price, and it is far better than a decision she regrets.

How long will it take?

Longer than clearing the same attic in an empty house, because every box is looked at. We tell you how long to allow when we send the quote, and there is time for a break.

The attic has no floor and the ladder is old. Can you still do it?

Yes. Our crew works from the joists and keeps its weight off the ceiling below. Tell us about the ladder when you send photos, so the crew can plan how the attic is reached.

The house is up a steep drive. Is that a problem?

No. Send a photo of the drive from the road. If the truck cannot reach the door, we carry down to it, and that is in the quote from the start.

Can the garage or a spare room be done another time?

Yes. Many families clear one space at a time. Each is quoted on its own, and there is no need to decide about the rest of the house now.
