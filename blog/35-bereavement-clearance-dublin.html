What Bereavement House Clearance Involves

Bereavement house clearance means emptying the home of someone who has died: their furniture, belongings, kitchen, wardrobes, and everything stored in the attic, garage and shed, so that the property can be sold, handed back, let or simply closed up. It is the same physical work as any house clearance. What makes it different is everything around that work.

The people we deal with are usually grieving. Often they are also an executor juggling a solicitor, an estate agent, siblings with different views, a property that needs heating and insuring, and a job they can't take any more time off from. Some live in another county or another country. A good bereavement clearance takes as much of that weight off as possible: one conversation, a clear quote, a date that suits, and a house that is empty and swept at the end of it, with the things that matter set aside rather than lost.

House Clearance After a Death in Dublin

Clearing a house after someone dies is rarely just a question of logistics. Every drawer can hold a memory, and decisions that look small from the outside, like what to do with a coat that still hangs in the hall, can stop you in your tracks. It is completely normal to find it overwhelming, to put it off, or to start and then need to stop.

Most of the people who contact us are clearing a parent's house: the family home they grew up in, where their mum or dad lived for thirty, forty or fifty years. Others are clearing the home of a grandparent, an aunt or uncle, a sibling, or a partner. Each situation is different, but the questions tend to be the same:

  • Where do we even start? With what you want to keep. Everything else can follow, and that part is our job.
  • Do we have to do it ourselves first? No. Some families go through the house carefully before we arrive; others take a few things and leave the rest to us. Both are fine.
  • Is it too soon? There is no right time. Some families need the property emptied quickly for a sale or a landlord; others wait until after probate or until they feel ready.
  • What happens to everything? Usable items go to reuse and donation where possible, materials are recycled, and only what's left goes to a licensed waste facility. More on that below.

Emptying a house after a bereavement on your own usually means weekends of sorting, trips to recycling centres, finding someone to take the big furniture, and dealing with an attic or garage nobody has looked into for years. A professional clearance does the heavy part in a day or two, usually in a single visit, so the family's time and energy can go where it is needed.

Sorting a Loved One's Belongings

The hardest part of clearing a home is rarely the furniture. It is the small things: the letters in a biscuit tin, the photographs at the back of a wardrobe, a watch in a bedside drawer. Before anything is cleared, we agree with you what needs to be kept, and we keep an eye out for it as we work.

If there are particular items you already know you want, such as a piece of furniture, a box of photographs or a set of china, tell us beforehand and we'll treat them that way from the start. If you'd like to be there to look through things as we go, that is welcome too. Many families ask us to hold off on one room until they've had a chance to go through it, and that's no problem.

Things we set aside for the family

As we clear, anything that looks personal, important or potentially valuable is put to one side for you to look through rather than cleared with everything else. That usually includes:

  • Photographs, photo albums and slides
  • Letters, cards and diaries
  • Documents and paperwork: wills, deeds, bank and pension papers, certificates
  • Jewellery, watches and small boxes
  • Cash, keys and anything in envelopes
  • Religious items, medals and family memorabilia
  • Collectables and items that look old or unusual
  • Anything you've named in advance

Important paperwork matters for the estate as well as for the family. The executor and solicitor may need bank statements, insurance policies, share certificates or a missing will, so keeping papers together is always the safer choice. If you think something might be valuable, such as antiques, art or a collection, it's worth having it valued before the clearance. We're happy to work around a valuer's visit.

What happens to furniture, clothing and household items

For everything the family doesn't need, we follow a simple order: reuse first, recycling second, disposal last.

  • Furniture in good condition, such as wardrobes, tables, chairs and sofas, is set aside for reuse or donation wherever possible. Older or damaged pieces are broken down so the wood and metal can be recycled.
  • Clothing, shoes and bedding in good condition go for reuse; the rest goes to textile recycling where available.
  • Books that are in good condition go to reuse where possible; the rest is recycled as paper.
  • Kitchen contents, like crockery, pots, pans and small appliances, are sorted for reuse and recycling.
  • Appliances and electrical items such as fridges, washing machines, cookers and TVs go to the proper electrical recycling routes.
  • Everything else goes to a licensed waste facility. Nothing is dumped.

If your family would like particular items donated, for example to a charity your loved one supported, let us know and we'll prioritise it. Many families tell us it makes the process feel a little less final to know things have gone to someone who can use them. We aim for an average recycling rate of up to 95% across everything we clear; the exact figure depends on what's in the house.

Probate and Deceased Estate Clearance in Dublin

Most bereavement clearances overlap with estate administration in some way. The house may be part of the estate, an executor may be waiting on a grant of probate, a solicitor may be handling the paperwork, and an estate agent may be waiting for the property to be empty before photos and viewings. Whether it's described as a probate clearance, an estate clearance or a deceased estate clearance, our job is the practical part: clearing the property at the right time, in the right way, without adding to anyone's workload.

That can mean a single phone call to arrange keys and a date, or working alongside a solicitor's office on timing over several weeks. Some executors prefer to leave the property untouched until certain administrative steps are complete; others want it cleared early to cut down on insurance, heating and security worries. Neither approach is wrong. We are happy to liaise directly with whoever is managing the estate and to work to whatever has been agreed.

Please note: we are a clearance company, not a legal practice. We don't give legal, tax or probate advice and we don't handle probate paperwork. Questions such as whether items can be disposed of before a grant of probate, or how household contents should be valued, are for the executor and the solicitor handling the estate. Once a plan has been agreed, we'll work to it.

If you have recently been named executor, our executor's checklist for Ireland walks through the wider process, and our guide on clearing a house before probate covers what is usually fine to do early and what is worth checking first.

Inherited property and getting a house ready for sale

When a family inherits a house, the next step is usually to sell it, let it, or move into it. In each case the contents need to go first. For a sale, estate agents generally prefer an empty, swept house for photographs and viewings; buyers find it easier to picture themselves in a clear space, and a sale can't close while the previous owner's belongings are still inside.

If you're working towards a listing date, a closing date or a handover to tenants, tell us the deadline and we'll tell you honestly whether we can meet it. We can work directly with the estate agent on access, and leave the house empty and swept, ready for the next step.

When Family Live Abroad or Can't Travel

Many of the families we work with are spread out. A son in London, a daughter in Australia, a brother in Galway, all trying to settle a parent's house in Dublin with limited time and a lot of phone calls. Not being able to get to the house shouldn't hold everything up.

A bereavement clearance can be arranged almost entirely at a distance:

  • Photos and video: a neighbour, friend, relative or the estate agent can send us photos or a short video of each room on WhatsApp, and we'll quote from those.
  • Keys: we can collect keys from a solicitor, estate agent, neighbour or key safe, and return them afterwards.
  • Clear instructions: tell us what to keep, where to leave it, and who will collect it. A short list or a few photos marking the items is enough.
  • Updates: we can keep in touch on WhatsApp, which works across time zones, and send photos once the property is clear.

If several family members need to agree on what's kept, it often helps to make that decision first and send us one combined list. That way nobody is caught out on the day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is bereavement house clearance?

It's the clearing of a home after the person who lived there has died: furniture, personal belongings, household contents, and any attic, garage, shed or storage unit. The work is the same as any house clearance, but it is planned around the family: items are set aside for you to look through, the timing works around probate, a sale or your own readiness, and everything is handled with care.

How much does bereavement house clearance cost in Dublin?

It depends mainly on how much there is to clear, which in practice means the number of van loads, plus the size of the property, access, and any attic, garage, shed or storage unit. We don't use a fixed price list. Send us photos or a short video and we'll give you a free, no-obligation quote.

Can I send photos or a video instead of booking a visit?

Yes, and it's the quickest way to get a quote. A few photos of each room, or a walk-through video including the attic, garage and shed, sent on WhatsApp to 083 090 4545, is usually enough. For very large or complicated properties we may arrange a quick look in person first.

How do you clear a house after someone dies?

We agree with you what needs to be kept and what can go, arrange a date and access, then clear the property room by room, including the attic, garage and shed. Anything personal, important or potentially valuable is set aside for the family. Usable items go to reuse and donation where possible, materials are recycled, and the rest goes to a licensed waste facility. The rooms are left empty and swept.

Can you clear my parent's house after they've passed away?

Yes. Clearing a parent's home is the most common bereavement clearance we do. We can clear the whole house or only the rooms you choose, and we'll work around whatever the family needs, whether that's going through things first, taking certain items, or leaving it all to us.

Can we go through the house ourselves before you clear it?

Yes, take whatever time you need. We're happy to arrange the clearance for whenever you're ready, to hold off on particular rooms, and to set aside anything you'd like to look through first.

Do we need to be at the property during the clearance?

Not unless you'd like to be. Some families prefer to be present throughout; others give us access and a list of instructions and let us get on with it. Either works, and you're welcome to change your mind on the day.

Can family members living abroad arrange a clearance?

Yes. Someone local, such as a neighbour, relative or the estate agent, can send us photos or a video, we can collect keys from a solicitor, estate agent or key safe, and we keep in touch on WhatsApp. We can send photos once the property is clear.

Can you work with a solicitor, executor or estate agent?

Yes. We regularly coordinate directly with whoever is handling the estate or the sale on timing, access, keys and any specific instructions for the property.

Can you clear a property while probate is still ongoing?

Many families and executors choose to have a property cleared before probate is finalised, particularly when a sale is being planned around it. Whether that's right for your situation is a question for your solicitor, not us. Once you and your solicitor have agreed a plan, we're happy to work to it.

Do you handle probate or legal paperwork?

No. We're a clearance company, so we don't give legal, tax or probate advice or deal with probate paperwork. We set aside any documents we find for the family and executor, and we work to whatever plan has been agreed with the solicitor handling the estate.

Can you clear a house before it is sold or let?

Yes. Many bereavement clearances are done so a house can go on the market, close a sale or be handed to tenants. Tell us the deadline and we'll tell you honestly whether we can meet it. We can also coordinate access directly with the estate agent.

Can you clear an apartment after a bereavement?

Yes. We clear apartments and flats across Dublin, including top-floor flats with no lift, buildings where the lift has to be booked, and developments with management company rules about access and timing. We plan around those details before the day.

Do you clear the attic, garage and shed too?

Yes, as part of the same job. Attics, garages and sheds often hold as much as the rest of the house, so please include them in your photos or video so the quote covers everything. We can also take down an old shed if needed.

Can you clear a storage unit that belonged to the person who died?

Yes. Once you've arranged access with the storage facility, we can clear the unit within their opening hours, keep aside anything the family wants, and leave it empty so the account can be closed.

What happens to furniture, clothing and books?

Anything in good condition is set aside for reuse or donation wherever possible. Clothing and textiles go to reuse or textile recycling, books to reuse or paper recycling, and furniture that can't be reused is broken down for recycling. Only what's left goes to a licensed waste facility.

Can items be donated rather than disposed of?

Yes. If donation matters to your family, let us know and we'll prioritise it for anything in suitable condition. If there is a particular charity your loved one supported, tell us and we'll do our best to route items there.

What happens if you find photographs, documents or jewellery?

We put them aside for the family rather than clearing them. Personal papers, photographs, jewellery, cash, keys and anything that looks important or valuable is kept together and left where you ask, or handed to whoever you nominate.

Do you take appliances and electrical items?

Yes. Fridges, freezers, washing machines, cookers, TVs and small electrical items are removed and taken to the appropriate electrical recycling routes.

Can you remove garden furniture, tools and garden waste?

Yes. Garden furniture, tools, lawnmowers and outdoor equipment are cleared with the house, and if the garden needs cutting back before a sale we can include green waste removal in the same visit.

Most homes are cleared in a single day. A large house with a full attic, garage and shed may take two days. We'll give you a realistic idea of timing with your quote, and if you'd prefer the work split across days, that's fine too.
