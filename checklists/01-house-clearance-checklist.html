House Clearance Checklist

Use this checklist to plan a house clearance from the first decision to the final walk-through. It works for a family home, a rental property or a house being prepared for sale. Tick items off as you go (your ticks are saved on this device), print it, or save it as a PDF.

1. Before You Book

  • Decide what the clearance covers: whole property, certain rooms, or the attic, garage and shed as well.
  • Decide who is making the decisions, and who the clearance team should contact.
  • Agree with everyone involved what is being kept, and what is not. If it is an estate, check with the solicitor first (see our guide to clearing before probate).
  • Find and set aside documents, valuables, keys and anything sentimental.
  • Take photos or a short video of every room, and of the attic, garage, shed, stairs and the front of the property.
  • Note access: stairs, lift, parking, narrow hallways, a long carry to the van.
  • Note any heavy or awkward items: wardrobes that do not come apart, pianos, safes, large appliances.
  • Note any hazardous items: paint, chemicals, gas bottles, batteries, anything you are unsure about.

2. Choosing a Company

  • Ask for the waste collection permit number and check it on the NWCPO permit search.
  • Ask for proof of public liability insurance.
  • Get a quote in writing, and ask what it includes (disposal, VAT, number of people, access assumptions).
  • Ask what happens to your items, and whether anything can be donated or recycled.
  • Ask what happens if the job turns out bigger or smaller than agreed.

Our guide to choosing a house clearance company has twelve questions to ask.

3. A Week Before

  • Confirm the date, the time window and who is arranging access.
  • Arrange parking close to the door. If the street is restricted or paid, ask about the council's arrangements early.
  • If it is an apartment, tell the management company or landlord and ask about lift booking and any rules.
  • Mark or move items to keep. Put them in one room, or label them clearly.
  • Arrange donations with charities you want to use, and check what they accept.
  • Sort electricals, batteries and hazardous items, and tell the team about them.
  • Make sure keys, alarm codes and any padlock keys for the shed or garage are ready.

4. On the Day

  • Be there, or have someone there, if you want to point out what is staying. Otherwise arrange access in advance.
  • Walk through each room with the crew and confirm what is going.
  • Point out anything fragile, anything to keep and any area you want left as it is.
  • Check the attic, garage, shed and any outside storage are included.
  • Check the property is left swept and tidy.
  • Do a final walk-through before the crew leaves.

5. After the Clearance

  • Check you have all the keys and nothing to keep has been removed.
  • Take final meter readings if you are handing back or selling the property.
  • Redirect the post if needed.
  • Arrange any cleaning, repairs or painting that comes next.
  • Keep the quote and any messages for your records.
